translation: >-
  Royal mung, and likewise *pītā*, lablab beans, and chickpeas too; black pigeon pea, and black gram, lentils, cowpeas: these seven are to be employed by soup-cooks in the making of soup. These are to be cooked split or unsplit, according to taste. Chickpeas, cowpeas, lentils, and royal mung are to be made split with querns by the discerning, for the sake of cooking; and the pigeon peas, bruised a little, are made twofold by the turnings of the mill. And when they have been properly made into split halves, they are freed of husk with winnowing-baskets. Having put cold water into the pot, equal in measure to the split pulse, he should strew the pulse in, and then set it on the stove. While it is cooking on a gentle fire he should throw asafoetida-water into it. For the sake of colour he should employ a little turmeric powder in it. Again and again he should add water, until the cooking is complete. Making rock-salt very fine, he should put it in at a twentieth part. By colour, by taste, by aroma, by softness, and by lightness too — thus is declared the proper perfection of the cooking of split pulse. Lablab beans and dark pigeon peas, kept free of asafoetida and unsplit, are to be cooked as before, without turmeric powder. In the cooking of lentils and black gram he should throw in asafoetida-water.

## Ingredients

* 250 g split mung, red lentils, split chickpeas, or urad dal  
* 900 ml water, plus more as needed  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* ½ tsp ground turmeric  
* 2 tsp fine rock salt, plus more to taste

## Method

1. Rinse the pulse until the water runs mostly clear.  
2. Combine the pulse, water, asafoetida, and turmeric in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a very gentle simmer.  
3. Cook partly covered until soft, adding small amounts of hot water whenever the mixture becomes too thick: about 20 minutes for red lentils, 30–40 minutes for split mung, or 45–60 minutes for split chickpeas.  
4. Stir in the salt, and serve.
