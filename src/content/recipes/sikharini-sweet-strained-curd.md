A simple ancestor of sweet strained-yoghurt dishes such as shrikhand. The curd being pressed is the *dadhi* of 1568–70, set from milk reduced to half, so the source's version is a good deal richer than one made from ordinary yoghurt. 1596 gives the consistency: in the order of a royal meal the king *licks* the *śikhariṇī*, which places it in the *lehya* class of 1344, alongside the one-third stage of reduced milk.

## Ingredients

* 500 g full-fat plain yoghurt, or one batch (500 ml) of the *dadhi*  
* 55 g fine sugar  
* ½ tsp ground cardamom

## Method

1. Line a sieve with clean cloth, add the yoghurt, and drain in the refrigerator for 6–12 hours.  
2. Transfer the thick curd to a bowl and beat with sugar and cardamom until smooth.  
3. Chill before serving.

**Notes**

* **500 g, not 750 g.** 1568–70 sets 500 ml of *dadhi*, and *takra* (1571–72) and *majjikā* (1573) each want the whole batch as well. The three are alternative treatments of one setting of curd rather than a sequence, so 500 g is the working unit throughout the chain; an earlier draft here asked for more than the parent recipe can produce.  
* Expect around 240 g of pressed curd from 500 g of ordinary yoghurt. The text's reduced-milk *dadhi* gives more, because the long simmer denatures the whey proteins and the gel holds on to its water.  
* *Lihyāt* at 1596 — "he should lick the *śikhariṇī*" — puts this in the *lehya* class of 1344, whose yardstick is the *pāyasa* of 1375, "fit for licking". Drain it until it is thick enough to hold a shape, not spoonably loose.  
* The whey is not waste: 1575 turns it into *mastu*. Draining 500 g of yoghurt leaves roughly 260 ml of whey, and the richer *dadhi* rather less.  
* The source contains no saffron, nuts, or fruit; these can be added as later-style variations but are not part of the basic recipe.
