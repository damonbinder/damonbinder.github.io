A long-simmered bone-in mutton or goat broth, seasoned simply with asafoetida, ginger, and salt.

## Ingredients

* 1.5 kg bone-in lamb or goat shoulder, breast, neck, and ribs  
* 3 L water  
* 30 g fresh ginger, sliced or bruised  
* ½ tsp asafoetida  
* 2½ tsp rock salt, plus more to taste

## Method

1. Place the meat and bones in a large heavy pot with the water.  
2. Bring slowly to a boil and skim the surface.  
3. Add the ginger, asafoetida, and salt. Simmer very gently, covered, for 3–4 hours, until the meat falls easily from the bones.  
4. Remove the bones, return the meat to the broth, and adjust salt to taste.

**Notes**

* The source does not specify skimming, but it produces a cleaner modern broth.  
* The term *utkvāthita* is from *ut-kvath*, the *kvātha* or decoction family — 1509 opens *kāthayet suciraṃ kālaṃ*, "decoct it for a very long time". But the endpoint the text names is the meat's tenderness (*mārdavaṃ*), not any degree of reduction, so this is a long broth rather than a concentrate.  
* **There is no sour ingredient in this passage.** 1508 names exactly three additions — *hiṅgunā*, asafoetida; *ārdrakeṇa*, fresh ginger; *saindhavena*, rock salt — and nothing acidic. An earlier version of this recipe read a sour agent into that line and dropped the ginger; neither is in the Sanskrit. This dish sits between genuinely sour recipes on both sides of it in the chapter, which is presumably how the mix-up happened.  
* *Sthālyavaktre* (1508) is not a form I can pin down. Read as *sthālī* + *avaktra* it is a pot with no proper mouth, i.e. narrow-necked; read as a *bahuvrīhi* it is a vessel with a *sthālī* over its mouth, i.e. covered. Either way the instruction is to restrict the opening, which is why the simmer here is covered rather than partly covered — 3 L of water uncovered for four hours would reduce a long way past a *sūpa*.  
* The cut list at 1507 is specific and all forequarter on the bone: *kroḍadeśa*, the chest; the shoulder together with *aṃsakīkasa*, the shoulder bones; and *pārśvakulyā*, the ribs.  
* *Mṛdbhāṇḍe*, an earthen vessel. A heavy non-reactive pot carries it; unlined reactive metal will pick up metallic flavour from the souring agent over four hours.  
* 1509 closes *khyātaṃ śāstraviśāradaiḥ*, "renowned among those skilled in the treatises" — the text citing dietetic authority for the dish rather than claiming it as the king's own, as 1501 does.  
* All 6 half-lines of 1507–1509 scan clean at 16 syllables.
