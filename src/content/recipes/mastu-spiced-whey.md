The verse before this one presses curd in a cloth to make *śikhariṇī*; this one picks up the liquid that ran out of the cloth and turns it into a drink. *Mastu* is the ordinary Sanskrit word for whey, so what 1575 defines is not the whey but the seasoning of it — and the seasoning ends in smoke, not in a spoonful of asafoetida.

## Ingredients

* 250 ml fresh whey, drained from one batch of *śikhariṇī*  
* 6 g ghee  
* ½ tsp cumin seeds  
* 2 g rock salt (about ⅓ tsp)  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida, for the smoke  
* A small piece of lump charcoal

## Method

1. Warm the ghee in a small pan, add the cumin seeds, and let them sizzle for 20–30 seconds. Whisk the ghee and cumin into the whey along with the salt.  
2. Fumigate. Stand a small metal cup or bowl in the middle of the whey so its rim clears the surface. Light the charcoal until it glows, drop it into the cup, sprinkle the asafoetida over it with a few drops of ghee, and cover the whole vessel tightly for 4–5 minutes. Lift out the cup.  
3. Serve at room temperature or gently warmed.

**Notes**

* **The asafoetida is smoked, not stirred in.** `हिङ्गुधूपेन धूपितम्` — *hiṅgudhūpena dhūpitam* — is "fumigated with asafoetida-incense", and the chapter keeps three separate constructions for asafoetida that should not be collapsed: `हिङ्गुतोय` (asafoetida water, thrown into the pot, six times), `हिङ्गुधूप` (asafoetida as incense, three times), and `हिङ्गुसर्पिस्` (asafoetida with ghee, at 1400, where it is dripped onto the fumigant). Verse 1478 puts two of them in adjacent pādas — `हिङ्गुधूपेन वासयेत्` against `हिङ्गुतोयेन सिञ्चितम्` — so the distinction is the author's, not an artifact of translation. The method above is the *dhūngar* technique still used for smoking dal and raita.  
* If you would rather not handle a coal, blooming the asafoetida in the hot ghee at step 1 is the usual modern stand-in. It is a substitute for the technique, not the technique.  
* **The whey should come from drained *dadhi*, not from paneer.** The parent verse presses set curd, so this is the sour, thin whey left from hanging yoghurt — not the sweeter whey from acid-set boiled milk. One *śikhariṇī* batch yields about 250 ml, and curd set from reduced milk in the chapter's own way drains less freely than commercial yoghurt does, so do not count on more.  
* ***Mastu* is the only one of these preparations that is genuinely downstream.** *Takra* (1572), *majjikā* (1573), and *śikhariṇī* (1574) are three parallel treatments of one setting of curd, not a sequence — you choose one. This recipe is the exception: it takes the liquid that the *śikhariṇī* has to give up, so the two get made together or not at all.  
* Ghee in the text (`आज्य`) is mixed in rather than used as a frying medium, and it will set into flecks if the drink is chilled. Serve this at room temperature or warm.  
* The text specifies no quantities. The salt here is about 0.8%, in line with the chapter's own seasoned buttermilk at 1572.
