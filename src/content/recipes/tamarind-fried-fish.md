The oil-and-salt rub, turmeric rinse, and tamarind simmer didn't add much, so I'd recommend skipping them unless you want to be maximally authentic.

## Ingredients

* 500 g firm white fish fillets or steaks  
* 2 tsp salt, divided  
* *(Optional)* ½ tsp turmeric  
* *(Optional)* 30 g tamarind concentrate  
* 50 g wheat flour  
* Neutral oil, for the rub and for shallow-frying  
* ¼ tsp ground cardamom  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper

## Method

1. *(Optional)* Rub the fish with 15 ml oil and 1 tsp salt for 1–2 minutes. Rinse briefly in water mixed with turmeric, then pat completely dry.  
2. *(Optional)* Dilute the tamarind in 150 ml water. Simmer the fish in this liquid for 2–4 minutes, just until the exterior firms, then drain and cool.  
3. Dust every surface with wheat flour.  
4. Shallow-fry over medium-high heat, turning once, until browned and cooked through.  
5. Grind the cardamom, pepper, and remaining salt together and sprinkle over the hot fish.

## Notes

* **The oil-and-salt rub is aimed at whole scaled fish, which is probably why it did nothing here.** 1527 sets its endpoint as *yāvat picchalatāṃ yāti matsyagandhaś ca naśyati* — "until it goes slimy and the fishy smell is destroyed". The sliminess is something the rubbing *produces*, not something it removes: salt and friction lift the mucus off the skin, and the turmeric wash in 1528 is what then takes it away. On skinless fillets there is no mucus layer to lift, so the step has nothing to work on.
* This is eight verses, and two of them are missing from the recipe. 1529 gives an alternative to the tamarind-and-flour treatment — throw the drained fish into the vessels described earlier, take the pot off the fire after a short time, and perfume it — so the tamarind simmer belongs specifically to the deep-fried version rather than to fish in general. 1526, the other one, is broken.
* 1526 is defective in the transcription: the first half-line comes in at 14 syllables against *anuṣṭubh*'s 16 and breaks off mid-phrase. What survives is *pāṭhīna* — the scaleless sheatfish, *Wallago attu*, a big-headed river catfish — and *pṛthakkṛtvā śiraḥ pacet*, "having separated the head, cook it". So the verse looks like the passage's exception clause: 1524 opens with *scaly* fish and their scales, and 1526 handles the scaleless ones and says their heads are cooked separately rather than thrown away. This is the one part of the passage that needs collating against Shrigondekar's printed edition. Of the other 15 half-lines of 1524–1531, 14 scan clean at 16; only 1525c–d is short, and there by one syllable that comes straight back if the editorial sandhi in *antrāṇy apasārayet* is dissolved to *antrāṇi apasārayet*.
* *Prapava* (1529) is not in the lexica. It sits beside *āṇaka*, itself a term of art in this chapter usually rendered "pan", so it is some second kind of cooking vessel, described somewhere earlier in the chapter — *pūrvaprasiddheṣu*, "the previously established ones".
