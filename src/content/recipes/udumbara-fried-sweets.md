The whole recipe is half a verse, and it names no filling — because it does not need to. `समावेष्ट्य` has no expressed object, and the only thing available to supply one is the accusative that the previous line has just finished describing: the milk-moistened, ghee-cooked, sugared and spiced grains `कीसारसंज्ञितान्`. So the *udumbara* is a *kīsāra* fritter, wrapped in the *maṇḍaka* dough and fried.

## Ingredients

* 200 g *maṇḍaka* dough  
* 150 g cooled *kīsāra*  
* 500 ml neutral oil, for frying

## Method

1. Divide the dough and *kīsāra* each into 16 portions.  
2. Flatten a piece of dough, place the filling in the centre, and pinch the dough closed around it.  
3. Roll gently into a small round or fig shape about 3 cm across.  
4. Heat the oil to 175 °C and fry in batches until golden, 3–4 minutes.  
5. Drain and serve warm.

**Notes**

* **The *kīsāra* filling is required by the syntax, not guessed at.** The gerund `समावेष्ट्य` needs an object; the passage supplies no new accusative; the string of accusative participles immediately before it ends in the naming formula `कीसारसंज्ञितान्`. The chapter is explicit when it starts a dish from new material — the *pūrikā* in the very next hemistich opens `उत्क्वाथ्य विदलान् पिष्ट्वा` — and here it does not.  
* **The name is a size, and it argues for small ones.** `उदुम्बर` is the cluster fig, *Ficus racemosa*, which fruits in bunches on the trunk at 2–3 cm across. [Judgement call: the earlier version of this reconstruction made 10 pieces from 350 g of dough and filling, or about 35 g and 4 cm each. Sixteen pieces of about 22 g is closer to a fig. Nothing in the text gives a count.]  
* `सम-आ-वेष्ट्य` is "wrapped completely round", the strong form of the verb the *veṣṭikā* is named from; the filling is fully enclosed, not folded over.  
* `तैलेन पचेत्` is unmarked as to depth. The chapter is precise when it cares: `कटाहे तैलपूर्णे` at 1384 and 1396 is a cauldron full of oil, and `किंचित्प्रसारिते तैले` at 1390 is a film of oil spread over the pan. This verse says neither, so deep-frying is a choice.
