The same seasoned chickpea as the *veṣṭikā*, but with no wrapper at all: it goes straight onto a griddle smeared with oil, which is why the oil is specified here and nowhere in the *veṣṭikā*'s own clause. That makes this passage the earliest description of what is now the dosa — and made from pulses alone, with no rice anywhere in it. Black gram, cowpea, or dried peas may stand in for the chickpea.

## Ingredients

* 200 g split chickpeas (chana dal), or urad dal, cowpeas, or dried peas  
* 15 g fresh ginger, finely chopped  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* 1 tsp rock salt  
* Oil, for the griddle

## Method

1. Soak the dal for 4–6 hours, drain, and grind with just enough water to a smooth pourable batter, about the consistency of thick cream.  
2. Stir in the ginger, cumin, asafoetida, and salt. Use it straight away — the text gives this batter no resting or souring, unlike the *iḍerikā* batter six verses later.  
3. Heat a tawa or heavy frying pan over medium heat and smear it with oil.  
4. Pour on a ladleful and spread it out from the centre into a thin round. Cook until the underside is set and freckled, oil the top, turn, and cook the second side.  
5. Serve hot off the pan.

**Notes**

* **The dish is *dhosaka*, not *ghosaka*.** Shrigondekar's printed text has `धोसकान्` in both halves of 1393, with no variant recorded in his apparatus; an earlier transcription had `घोसकान्`, a `घ`/`ध` confusion. *Dhosaka* is the Sanskritized form of the word behind Tamil *tōcai* and Kannada *dose*, and Shrigondekar says as much in his own introduction, listing "Dhoṣa" and "Iḍali" among the chapter's dish names still current in Kannada and Tamil. The secondary literature accordingly reads this passage as the earliest recipe for the dosa. **Recommended title on publication: *Dhosaka*.**  
* **What the text does and does not specify about the pulse.** पूर्वसम्भारसंस्कृतम् sends the *dhosaka*'s pulse back to 1391's seasonings — asafoetida, cumin, salt, ginger — and nothing further. It gets no boiling or grinding verb of its own; the chapter supplies those two verses earlier, at the *pūrikā*'s उत्क्वाथ्य विदलान् पिष्ट्वा. [Judgement call: soaked-and-ground, because a boiled paste will not spread into a batter. The text distinguishes only the wrapper and the vessel from the *veṣṭikā* — see there for the parallel call on its own preparation.]  
* **वट्टाण is the pea**, *Pisum sativum* — the Prakrit form behind Marathi *vāṭāṇā*, and one of the several Deccan vernacular words this chapter carries. राजमाष is the cowpea or black-eyed pea, *Vigna unguiculata*; despite the modern Hindi *rājmā* it is not the kidney bean, which had not reached India.  
* Also collated: 1394c reads वट्टाणकस्य in the printed edition, not वट्टाणस्य as transcribed above at 1393cd, which is why that half-verse currently scans a syllable short.
