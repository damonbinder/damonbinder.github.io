The plant list is long and many names are regional or uncertain, but the principle is simple: tender leaves dressed sharply with tamarind, citrus, or yoghurt, and salt.

## Ingredients

* Tender sour leaves, whatever you can find — I used spinach
* Salt, to taste
* One dressing: tamarind concentrate diluted in a little water, or lime juice, or plain yoghurt

## Method

1. Wash and dry the leaves carefully. Tear larger leaves into bite-sized pieces.
2. Toss the leaves with salt and your chosen dressing immediately before serving.

## Notes

* Some two dozen plant names across four verses, and the list closes with *tathāparam*, "and another besides" — an open invitation rather than a fixed formula. About a third of the names cannot be identified with any confidence, and those are left out of the translation rather than guessed at.
* Many plant names in the source are regional or uncertain; use only leaves positively identified as edible, and buy them rather than gather them. *Bhallātaka* is the marking nut, *Semecarpus anacardium*, whose sap contains bhilawanol — a urushiol of the same class as the one in poison ivy — and blisters skin on contact; Ayurveda purifies the fruit before any internal use. *Kokilākṣī* is spiny, and flax carries cyanogenic glycosides. Several of the listed plants are drugs rather than vegetables.
