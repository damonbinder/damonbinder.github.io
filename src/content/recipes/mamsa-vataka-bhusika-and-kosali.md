These are stuffed meatballs, and the stuffing is the preceding recipe: the ember-roasted meat of 1476–77, chopped to the fineness of sesame seeds and seasoned with citron, ginger, and spice. Raw meat is wet-ground on the stone into a sticky paste, and that paste is closed around the roasted mince — cooked in a pan (*māṃsa-vaṭaka*) or deep-fried (*bhūṣikā*). *Kośalī* swaps the meat casing for wheat dough and roasts the same filling in embers.

So you need the [Roast-Meat Salad](/recipes/manasollasa/roast-meat-salad-with-citron-and-ginger/) made first. The filling is cooked and the casing is raw, which is the whole difficulty of the dish.

## Ingredients

For the filling

* 250 g of the Roast-Meat Salad, made as in 1476–77

For the casing

* 500 g finely minced lamb, goat, or pork  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida dissolved in 30 ml water  
* 1½ tsp salt  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ½ tsp ground coriander

Then, for one of the three

* 20 g ghee, for *māṃsa-vaṭakas*  
* 500 ml oil, for *bhūṣikās*  
* 250 g plain wheat dough, for *kośalīs* — and no casing paste at all

## Method

1. Work the mince with the asafoetida-water, salt, cumin, pepper, and coriander — by hand, in a mortar, or in a processor — until it is smooth, tacky, and holds together like a stiff dough. *Piṣṭavac cikaṇam*, sticky like dough, is the test.  
2. Divide the paste into 12. Flatten each piece in a wet palm, put about 20 g of the roast mince in the middle, close the paste over it, seal the seam, and roll it smooth. Keep the wall even; a thin patch splits in the pan.  
3. For *māṃsa-vaṭakas*, brown the balls in the ghee over medium heat, add 100 ml water, cover, and cook for 10–12 minutes, turning once, until the casing reads 71°C.  
4. For *bhūṣikās*, deep-fry the same balls at 165°C for 6–8 minutes, until deep brown and 71°C in the casing. Or cook them as in step 3 and then fry them briefly for color.  
5. For *kośalīs*, leave the paste out entirely. Roll the dough thin, enclose 40–50 g portions of the roast mince, seal the edges well, and roast in embers or bake at 220°C for 15–20 minutes, until the dough is browned and the filling is hot through.

**Notes**

* **The filling is the preceding recipe, and that is what makes the passage cohere.** 1480a–b reads *cūrṇīkṛtaṃ tu yanmāṃsaṃ golakais tad viveṣṭayet* — "the meat which has been reduced to *cūrṇa*, that (*tat*, accusative) one should envelop with the balls (*golakaiḥ*, instrumental)". The accusative is what gets enveloped and the instrumental is the wrapper, so the paste balls of 1479 are the casing and the *cūrṇa* meat is the core. 1480c then calls the results *cūrṇagarbhān vaṭakān*, "*cūrṇa*-cored *vaṭakas*". And the *cūrṇa* meat is not newly introduced here: 1476–77 has just cut ember-roasted meat *tilaśaḥ*, to the fineness of sesame seeds, and dressed it — that is a *cūrṇa* of meat, and 1482's *tad eva cūrṇitaṃ māṃsam*, "**that same** *cūrṇita* meat", points back to it. The *ca* of 1478c is the hinge: *āmaṃ māṃsaṃ **ca***, "**and** *raw* meat", sets the casing against the roasted meat just finished.
* **This is why the text never says how to season the core.** It doesn't need to — the core arrives already cooked and already seasoned from 1477. Reading the core as a second, separately-made raw mince forces you to invent both a proportion and a spicing that the text withholds, which is the sign the reading is wrong. The 500/250 split here is still mine; the text gives no proportions.
* ***Cūrṇa* here is minced meat, not spice powder.** The word does mean a ground spice elsewhere in the chapter, and 1479 uses it that way one line earlier (*lavaṇena ca cūrṇena saṃhitaṃ*, "joined with salt and with the powder"). But 1482 says *cūrṇitaṃ māṃsam* and 1485 says *cūrṇamāṃsena*, both naming meat outright — and an eggplant cannot be stuffed with dry spice.
* **1485 confirms the filling is this chapter's standing stuffing.** The vegetables of the next recipe are filled with *tena māṃsena*, "that meat", which 1485c then names *cūrṇamāṃsena*. Same filling, fourth wrapper.
* **The structure is one filling and four wrappers, then the unwrapped version.** Raw-paste ball in the pan (1480), the same fried (1481), wheat dough in embers (1482), a hollowed vegetable (1483–85) — and then *vaṭṭimaka* at 1486–87, which is the paste alone with nothing inside it. *Kośalī* is the proof that the filling is an independent thing: it keeps the filling and drops the meat casing entirely.
* **The two layers have opposite cooking requirements.** The casing is raw (*āmaṃ māṃsam*, said at both 1479 and 1486) and has to come up to temperature; the filling is already cooked, already dry from the roasting, and only needs heating through. So take the internal temperature of the casing, not the core, and keep the frying brisk — a long fry to "cook the middle" only dries out a middle that was never raw.
* *Kaṇikā* (1482) is wheat flour or wheat dough: the Marathi gloss is "wheaten flour, whether fine flour or meal", and the Kannada lexica give "dough made of wheat flour, a premix for making different food items". A dough parcel also suits the name, since *kośa* is a sheath or pod.
* *Bhūṣikā* is transparently from *bhūṣā*, ornament, and 1481 calls the *vaṭakas* *dṛśyā manoharāḥ*, handsome to look at and delightful — so the fried version may well be named for how it looks. 1481 also says *ta eva vaṭakāḥ*, "those very same *vaṭakas*", so the *bhūṣikās* are the same objects at the same size, not smaller ones. That is why the frying oil here runs a little cooler than usual: a 50 g ball at 175°C colors past brown before the casing is cooked.
* *Āṇaka*, the vessel of 1480, is not securely identified. The lexica give *āṇaka*/*ānaka* as a large drum rather than a pot, and the chapter always sets it against two other options — deep oil, and the bare fire (1487, 1521, 1532, 1539, where the fire is twice specified as smokeless). So it is a closed or drum-shaped vessel used with little fat. "Pan" is safe; a covered pan is probably closer, which is what step 4 uses.
* **The three dishes are alternatives, not a sequence.** A cook makes one. The ghee, the frying oil, and the dough are each for a single variant, and *kośalī* needs no paste at all.
* Check every ball. A filled ball is thicker than a plain one, and the seam is where it fails.
* **The passage shares verse 1478 with the preceding recipe, and the seam runs down the middle of it.** 1478a–b closes the *Roast-Meat Salad* — *tanmāṃsaṃ*, "that meat", points back to the ember-roasted meat of 1476a. 1478c–d, *āmaṃ māṃsaṃ ca peṣaṇyāṃ hiṅgutoyena siñcitam*, opens this recipe and has no finite verb of its own until *peṣayed* in 1479b. Both recipes print the whole verse and translate only their own half.
