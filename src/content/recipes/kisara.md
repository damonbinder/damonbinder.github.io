A sweet, rich preparation of coarse wheat grain toasted in ghee and spiced with cardamom and black pepper. The source calls for a deliberately coarse grain — the gritty fraction beaten out of the flour with a winnowing basket — which keeps the grains distinct rather than collapsing into a smooth paste. It is a dry, granular sweet, not a porridge: the text moistens the grain with milk and then cooks it in ghee, and never adds liquid a second time.

## Ingredients

* 150 g coarse semolina (coarse rava/sooji) or dalia (cracked wheat) — not fine semolina; see note  
* 100 ml whole milk  
* 60 g ghee  
* 75 g white sugar  
* ½ tsp ground cardamom  
* ¼ tsp finely ground black pepper  
* Pinch of salt

## Method

1. Moisten the coarse grain with the milk — enough to dampen it evenly and no more, so it stays crumbly rather than becoming a slurry. Leave it 10 minutes for the milk to soak in.  
2. Melt the ghee in a heavy pan over medium-low heat. Add the moistened grain and cook, stirring constantly, for 12–15 minutes, until it smells nutty, the grains stay loose and separate, and a grain crushed between the fingers is tender to the bite.  
3. Take the pan off the heat, stir in the sugar, cardamom, pepper, and salt, and return it to a low flame for two or three minutes to melt the sugar through.  
4. Serve warm in small portions.

**Notes**

* **Grain matters most.** The source calls for coarse wheat particles winnowed from flour, and grain size is what governs the texture. Fine semolina (standard fine sooji, farina, Cream of Wheat) absorbs liquid fast and welds into a lump in hot ghee — use a coarse rava, or dalia/cracked wheat, so the grains stay distinct. Bulgur works as a fallback but is par-cooked, so it cooks faster and comes out nuttier.  
* **The milk is a moistening, not a cooking liquid.** `दुग्धाक्तान्` is from *añj*, to smear or anoint — the grain is *dampened* with milk and then `घृतपक्व`, ghee-cooked. There is no second addition of milk anywhere in the verse and no porridge stage; the endpoint is closer to a rava ladoo mixture than to *shira*. [Judgement call: the earlier version of this reconstruction added 250 ml of milk in stages and offered a porridge finish. That is not in the text, and cutting it changes what the dish is. Coarse rava will cook through on 100 ml plus the ghee; dalia is whole wheat, takes more liquid and longer, and if you use it you will need to add water and accept something wetter.]  
* **The sugar is `सिता`, refined white sugar, not `शर्करा`.** The chapter distinguishes them: the *pūrikā* filling two verses later takes `शर्करा`, and 1411–16 describes at length how `शर्करा` is clarified with milk and strained through cloth to become `सिता`. So this is the more refined of the chapter's two sugars — white sugar, not jaggery.  
* The black pepper is explicit and should remain perceptible, though not dominant.  
* **The name.** `कीसार` is Shrigondekar's reading; his manuscript D has `कासार`, and the secondary literature usually cites the dish as *kāsāra*, grouping it with *udumbara* and the *varṣopala-golaka* of 1417 as the chapter's wheat sweets. Also collated: 1387a reads `घृतपक्वांश्च` in the printed edition, not `घृतपकांश्च` as transcribed above.  
* The coarse fraction this dish is made of is the by-product of sifting the *maṇḍaka* flour at 1376. The two recipes are meant to be made from one milling.
