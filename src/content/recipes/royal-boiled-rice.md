translation: >-
  The grains unbroken, thoroughly cleaned, washed many times with water, white as the *kunda* jasmine, and held a long while under water: in a pot made of copper, or else of clay, he should put water three times the rice, and have it covered with a cloth white as the moon, washed, and thick. Setting it on the stove, he should boil that water on a smokeless fire. When the water is thoroughly hot, covered with bubbles, and giving off a little steam, he should strew the rice into the pot and stir it about with a ladle. The discerning man should crush a grain and inspect it again and again. When that grain has gone soft, or still holds a little core, he should put milk into it, or else ghee, and then take it off the fire. Setting a woven lid over the mouth of the pot, the skilled man should let the starchy cooking water run off; and the little that is left behind he should dry away in the residual heat.

This uses the pasta method of cooking rice, which, perhaps to the surprise of many Asians, is not a European invention but is, in fact, how Indians traditionally (and, as this recipe shows, for at least the past thousand years) have always cooked rice. The recipe calls for adding the milk or ghee before draining, which doesn't make a lot of sense to me, so I've flipped the ordering. It also doesn't call for salt, but again, I would assume this is probably salted, although again, this is not entirely clear.

## Ingredients

* 300 g aged basmati or another fragrant long-grain rice  
* 1.1 L water, plus more for washing and soaking  
* 15 g ghee, or 60 ml whole milk  
* ½ tsp fine salt (optional)

## Method

1. Wash the rice until the water is nearly clear, then soak it for an hour and drain.  
2. Bring the 1.1 L of water to a boil in a large saucepan — the point the text describes, bubbling and just beginning to steam. Add the rice and stir once with a ladle.  
3. Boil uncovered for 6–9 minutes. Test often by pressing a grain between your fingers, and stop when the grains are tender, or tender with a faint firm centre.  
4. Drain immediately through a fine colander. Return the rice to the warm pan, add the ghee or milk, cover, and leave off the heat for 8 minutes.  
5. Fluff gently and serve hot.
