translation: >-
  Rice cooked in this way, fit for a king, is the best. Use *rāja-mudga*, yellow mung, *niṣpāva*, chickpeas, dark *āḍhakī*, black gram, lentils, or *rāja-māṣa*: these are the seven that soup-cooks employ [the list as transmitted gives eight names — "yellow" is probably a description of the *rāja-mudga* rather than a pulse of its own, since the seven that recur later in the passage do not include it]. Cook them split or whole according to taste. Chickpeas, *rāja-māṣa*, lentils, and *rāja-mudga* are split on the quern; the *āḍhakī* is first bruised a little and then halved by turning the mill. Split them properly and winnow off the husks. Put cold water in a pot in an amount equal to the split pulse, add the pulse, set it on the stove, and cook over a low fire, throwing in asafoetida-water while it cooks. For colour, add a little turmeric powder, and add water again and again until the cooking is complete. Add finely ground rock salt in the proportion of one part to twenty. So is declared the proper perfection of split-pulse cooking, judged by colour, taste, aroma, softness, and lightness. *Niṣpāva* and dark *āḍhakī* are cooked whole, as before, without asafoetida or turmeric. In cooking lentils and black gram, put in asafoetida-water.

## Ingredients

* 250 g split mung, red lentils, split chickpeas, or urad dal  
* 300 ml cold water, plus about 600 ml more added during cooking  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* ½ tsp ground turmeric  
* 12 g fine rock salt, plus more to taste

## Method

1. Rinse the pulse until the water runs mostly clear.  
2. Put the 300 ml of cold water in a heavy saucepan, add the pulse, and set it on the heat. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a very gentle simmer.  
3. Stir the asafoetida into a couple of tablespoons of water and add it, then add the turmeric.  
4. Cook partly covered until soft, adding hot water in small amounts whenever the mixture becomes too thick — about 600 ml in all: about 20 minutes for red lentils, 30–40 minutes for split mung, or 45–60 minutes for split chickpeas. The first top-up comes within the first ten minutes; stir the base so it does not catch.  
5. Stir in the salt, and serve.

## Notes

* The salt is the text's own proportion: a twentieth part, which for 250 g of pulse is about 12 g. In a finished soup of roughly a kilogram that lands near 1.1% salt — ordinary seasoning, and about twice what a cook reaches for by habit.  
* The text starts with cold water equal in measure to the pulse and then tops it up again and again until the pulse is done. The total is the same as adding it all at once; the difference is that the dal is thick from the start and finishes creamier.  
* Asafoetida goes in as *hiṅgu-toya*, dissolved in water rather than sprinkled dry, and turmeric goes in for colour alone.  
* The passage closes with a five-point standard for a well-cooked dal: colour, taste, aroma, softness, and lightness.  
* *Āḍhakī* is the pigeon pea (*toor*, *arhar*) and *rāja-māṣa* the cowpea (*lobiā*). *Niṣpāva* is the lablab or hyacinth bean (*sem*), which is why it is cooked whole rather than split. *Rāja-mudga* is not securely identified and is left transliterated.
