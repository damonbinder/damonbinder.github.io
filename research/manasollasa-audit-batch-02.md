# Mānasollāsa 3.13.1385–1401 — the wheat sweets and pulse cakes audited

*Created 2026-07-25 12:16.*

Scope: seven recipes covering 3.13.1385–1401 — `patrika-layered-breads.md`, `kisara.md`, `udumbara-fried-sweets.md`, `sweet-pulse-purika.md` (**published**), `savoury-vestika-and-ghosaka.md`, `katakarna-pulse-fritters.md`, `iderika-fermented-black-gram-cakes.md`. All seven were edited. `src/data/sources/manasollasa.ts` was not touched.

## Summary

**Six of seven recipes had substantive errors, and I was able to collate the whole passage against Shrigondekar's Gaekwad's Oriental Series edition directly** — Vol. II, Baroda 1939, printed pages 118–120, read off the scan. That changes the character of this audit: it settles the two questions the brief flagged as open, and it turns three apparent metrical corruptions into three plain transcription slips.

**The most consequential correction: the dish this collection calls *ghosaka* is Shrigondekar's `धोसकान्` — *dhosaka*, the Sanskritized *dosa* — and it has no dough wrapper at all.** The printed text has `धोसकान्` in both halves of 1393 with no variant in the apparatus; our transcription has `घोसकान्`, a `घ`/`ध` confusion. Shrigondekar says as much in his own introduction, listing "Dhoṣa" and "Iḍali" among the chapter's dish names still current in Kannada and Tamil, and the secondary literature accordingly reads this verse as the earliest recipe for the dosa. The reconstruction had it as a second stuffed flatbread, near-identical to the *veṣṭikā* — and then apologised for the overlap in a note. The actual contrast is sharp: the *veṣṭikā* is wrapped in dough and cooked on an earthen `खर्पर`; the *dhosaka* is the same seasoned pulse spread bare onto an oil-smeared `ताप्या`, which is exactly why the oil appears in the second clause and not the first.

**On `kaṇḍu`: the leaven was invented, and it is gone.** `कण्डुना परिघट्टितम्` is an implement in the instrumental — the same construction as `दर्व्या परिघट्टयेत्` at 1354 — and on either live reading (`कन्दु`, an iron pan in Suśruta, giving `कान्दविक` "a baker" and Kannada *kaṃdu* "frying pan"; or an instrument-noun from √*kaṇḍ*, to husk or pound) it is a tool, not a starter. Nothing in 1394–97 sours or rests the mixture. The contrast with the *iḍerikā* three verses later is decisive, because there the text says both things explicitly: `स्थापयेत्तदहः` and `आम्लीभूतं`. Two independent points make the fermentation not just unsupported but incoherent: `चूर्णितं` means the pulses are **dry-milled to flour** and then slaked with water, so there is no soak in which a wild population could establish itself; and `बुद्बुदसंकाशा`, the "bubble-like" appearance the reconstruction was reaching for, is a doneness cue for deep frying, matching `यावल्लौहित्यं तेषु जायते` for the *ghārikā* six verses later.

**On whether the *iḍerikā* is steamed: yes, and the collation supplies the missing word.** Shrigondekar reads 1399c as `वस्त्रगर्भाभिरन्याभिः` — "with others having cloth inside", i.e. the covering moulds are **cloth-lined**, which is how idli moulds are still lined. Our transcription lost `वस्त्र-`, which is also exactly why that half-verse scanned two syllables short. Three further arguments converge: the finished cakes are `धवलाः`, white, where the same batter deep-fried in the next verse comes out `लौहित्यं`, red, and only moist heat keeps a pulse batter white; `परिपाचयेत्` is "cook right through, all round"; and the scholarly summary of this passage renders it as placed in moulds and "cooked by steam properly". The hedge in the note ("the text does not explicitly say steam") has been replaced with the argument. Since the *ghārikā* and the two *vaṭaka* recipes descend from this batter, the parent is now firm.

**Before cooking, two things Damon should know.** The *kaṭakarṇa* is a different dish from what the file described — a thin, hard, bubbled cracker from a dry-flour dough, not a soft leavened fritter — and `निष्पाव` is lablab (*val*/*sem*), not fava, which is the third instance of that same misidentification in the chapter. And the *kīsāra* reconstruction was adding 250 ml of milk in stages and offering a porridge finish; the text moistens the grain once and never adds liquid again, so it is a dry granular sweet closer to a rava ladoo mixture. That change alters what the dish is, so it is flagged inline in the file as a judgement call.

## 1. The collation against Shrigondekar

I downloaded the GOS Vol. II scan and read printed pages 118–120 (PDF pages 167–169) at 300 dpi. Every verse from 1384 to 1403 is legible, including the critical apparatus. This is the collation the previous audit recommended for 1427–35, done for this stretch instead.

**The passage is not corrupt. The working transcription has drifted in eight places.** None of them is an editorial crux; all eight are ordinary copying slips, and three of them are what produced the metrical defects.

| Verse | `original` as transcribed | Shrigondekar GOS II, pp. 118–119 | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1385b | `मृदवः` | `मृद्वः` (with the editor's own `(द्वः)`) | metre: 17 → 16 |
| 1385d | `ताप्य` | **`ताप्यां`** (MS A `ताप्यान्`) | **locative of *tāpī*, "on the griddle" — not a gerund "having heated"** |
| 1386a | `निक्षिप्ताः पत्रिकाः` | `निक्षिप्ता(ः) पत्रिका` | orthographic only |
| 1387a | `घृतपकांश्च` | `घृतपक्वांश्च` | correct Sanskrit *ghṛta-pakva*, "ghee-cooked" |
| 1387d | `कीसार-` | `कीसार-`, MS D `कासार-` | the secondary literature cites the dish as *kāsāra* |
| 1393a, 1393d | `घोसकान्` ×2 | **`धोसकान्` ×2, no variant** | **the dish is *dhosaka*; see §5** |
| 1394c | `वट्टाणस्य` | **`वट्टाणकस्य`** | metre: 15 → 16 |
| 1399c | `गर्भाभिरन्याभिः` | **`वस्त्रगर्भाभिरन्याभिः`** | metre: 14 → 16, **and the covering moulds are cloth-lined** |

Two negative results worth having, because they close off speculation: **`कण्डुना` (1395d) and `वैटिकासु` (1399a) are printed exactly as transcribed, with no variants recorded.** So neither word is a transmission problem; they are genuinely obscure words that the manuscripts agree on.

Also confirmed as matching the edition: `हरिमन्थस्य विदलं`, `वेष्टयित्वा गोलकेन`, `पूर्वसम्भारसंस्कृतम्`, `किंचित्प्रसारिते तैले`, `सैन्धवेन च संयुक्तं`, `निष्पावचूर्णसंयुक्तं`, `कटाहे तैलसंपूर्णे`, `यावद्बुद्बुदसंकाशा`, `स्थापयेत्तदहस्ततः`, `आम्लीभूतं माषपिष्टं`, all of 1400, and `सुशीताः धवलाः श्लक्ष्णा एता इडेरिका वराः`. At 1393a the edition prints `तैले⁶(ल) विलिप्तायां` — the manuscripts have `तैले`, the editor corrects to `तैल-` — and our transcription has silently adopted the editor's correction, which is fine but worth knowing.

**Recommendation for the central re-sync:** apply all eight to `manasollasa.ts` and to the four affected recipes' `original` fields. I did not touch any `original` field. One more for the sweep, outside my range: 1381a `चतस्रश्च चतस्रो घटिता मण्डका वराः` scans at 15 in our transcription too, so `ember-baked-angarapolika-and-griddle-polika.md` probably has a ninth slip.

## 3. *Patrikā* Layered Breads (1385cd–1386ab)

### Confirmed

- **P1. `ताप्यां` — the griddle was lost.** The edition's locative makes these griddle breads, which is the whole point of their position in the text: they follow two deep-fried breads (`कटाहे तैलपूर्णे`, the *sohalā* and *pāhalikā*) and the same word `ताप्यां` recurs at 1390 and 1393. The transcription's `ताप्य` reads as a gerund and the translation dropped it altogether. Restored to the translation, and the collation is noted in the file.
- **P2. The dough is a dropped back-reference.** `गोलान्` at 1385c is bare and anaphoric: `गोलक` in this chapter means the *maṇḍaka* dough-ball of 1377–78, enriched with milk (`क्षीरनीरेण पिण्डितम्`) and a little ghee, dusted with rice flour, and it recurs as `गोलान्`/`गोलकान्` at 1381, 1382, 1389, and 1392. The reconstruction had re-derived a plain flour-salt-water dough. Every sibling recipe in the cluster — *sohalā*, *aṅgārapolikā*, *udumbara*, *pūrikā*, *veṣṭikā* — already says "*maṇḍaka* dough", so this file was the only holdout. **Fixed:** ingredient list now points at the *maṇḍaka* dough, and the intro says why.
- **P3. `विपचेत्` and `सुधीः` dropped.** The finite verb and the "wise cook" epithet were both absent from the translation. Restored.

### Judgement calls

- **P-J1. The stack is cooked again.** `विपचेत्` governs `पत्रिकाः`, which is what `उपर्युपरि निक्षिप्ताः` agrees with; the earlier `पाचितान्` agrees with `गोलान्` instead. Different genders, so the syntax does separate the two operations: the balls are spread thin and fat-cooked, and then the *patrikās*, stacked, are cooked. I added a step returning the stack to the griddle. Read the other way, `विपचेत्` is just the main verb for the whole operation and the sheets are stacked for service. Flagged inline with `[Judgement call: …]`; delete step 4 to revert. This replaces the old note ("the source does not state whether the sheets were fused into one cake"), which was a fair hedge but ignored the verb order.

Also added: *patrikā* means "little leaf", so the name is about thinness; and the text says `स्नेहेन`, "with fat", not specifically ghee.

## 4. *Kīsāra* (1386cd–1387)

### Confirmed

- **K1. The winnowing clause was half-carried.** `गोधूमचूर्णादुद्धृत्य शूर्पेणाभ्याहतान् कणान्` — "taking from the wheat flour the grains beaten out with a winnowing basket" — is the sentence that says what this dish is made of, and the translation reduced it to the noun phrase "coarse wheat particles". Restored in full. See §2 for the boundary reasoning.
- **K2. `सिता` is not generic sugar.** The chapter distinguishes `शर्करा` from `सिता` and spends 1411–16 describing how the first becomes the second — clarified with milk, strained repeatedly through clean cloth. The *pūrikā* filling two verses later takes `शर्करा`; the *kīsāra* takes `सिता`. So this is the refined white sugar, and the choice is deliberate. Translation now reads "white sugar"; ingredient list matches; a note explains.
- **K3. The second milk addition is not in the text.** `दुग्धाक्तान्` is from *añj*, to smear or anoint, and it is a moistening; the grain is then `घृतपक्व`, ghee-cooked; then sugared; then spiced. There is no further liquid anywhere in the verse and no porridge stage. **Fixed:** milk cut from ~250 ml to 100 ml, step 3's staged additions and the porridge option removed, and the frying step given a real endpoint (12–15 minutes, until a crushed grain is tender).

### Judgement calls

- **K-J1. Dropping the porridge finish changes the dish.** It becomes a dry granular sweet, texturally a rava ladoo mixture rather than *shira*. This is what the text describes, but it is a real change to a reconstruction that presumably read plausibly before, so it is flagged inline. The old note "keep the milk light at the start" was pointing the same way already; the difference is that the text now supplies the reason rather than the technique supplying it. Coarse rava will cook through on 100 ml plus 60 g of ghee; dalia will not, and the note says so.

Also added: the `कीसार`/`कासार` variant and the 1387a `घृतपक्व` collation, and the point that the coarse fraction this dish uses is the by-product of sifting the *maṇḍaka* flour at 1376, so the two recipes come from one milling.

## 5. *Udumbara* Fried Sweets (1388ab)

### Confirmed

- **U1. The *kīsāra* filling is required by the syntax, not inferential.** The note said "the connection between udumbara and kīsāra is inferential; the text may instead refer to another filling lost in compression." It is not. `समावेष्ट्य` is a gerund with no expressed object; the passage introduces no new accusative; and the accusative string immediately preceding closes on the naming formula `कीसारसंज्ञितान्`. The chapter is explicit when a dish starts from new material — the *pūrikā* in the very next hemistich opens `उत्क्वाथ्य विदलान् पिष्ट्वा चणकप्रभृतीन्`. **Fixed:** translation now names the referent with a bracket, intro gives the argument, note replaced.
- **U2. The translation was a paraphrase, not a translation.** "A sweet preparation is enclosed in a ball of dough and fried in oil" dropped `सम-आ-` (wrapped completely round, the strong form of the verb the *veṣṭikā* is named from) and the finite verb. Restored.

### Judgement calls

- **U-J1. Fig-sized means smaller pieces.** `उदुम्बर` is *Ficus racemosa*, which fruits in clusters on the trunk at 2–3 cm. The name is the only shape guidance the half-verse gives, and 10 pieces from 350 g of dough and filling is ~35 g and ~4 cm each. I changed it to 16 pieces, about 22 g. Nothing in the text gives a count; flagged inline.

Also noted: `तैलेन पचेत्` is unmarked as to frying depth, where the chapter is precise when it cares (`कटाहे तैलपूर्णे` at 1384 and 1396 versus `किंचित्प्रसारिते तैले` at 1390), so the deep-fry is a choice rather than the text's instruction.

## 6. Sweet Pulse *Pūrikā* (1388cd–1390) — **published**

Three edits, all inside the `translation` field, plus a `related` array. The body prose, ingredients, and method are untouched.

### Confirmed

- **Q1. `किंचित्प्रसारिते` modifies the oil, not the pastry.** `किंचित्प्रसारिते तैले` is locative singular agreeing with locative singular `तैले` — "in oil spread out a little", a film of oil over the pan. The translation attached it to the parcel instead ("flatten it slightly"). The grammatical agreement is locked, and the chapter's contrast between this and `कटाहे तैलपूर्णे`/`कटाहे तैलसंपूर्णे` (1384, 1396) is exactly the shallow-versus-deep distinction. **The method was already right** — it says "Shallow-fry in neutral oil" — so this fixes a translation that contradicted Damon's own cooking note.

## 7. Savoury *Veṣṭikā* and *Ghosaka* (1391–1394ab) — the *dhosaka*

This is the recipe that needed rebuilding rather than patching.

### Confirmed

- **V1. `धोसकान्`, not `घोसकान्` — and the dish is the dosa.** See §1 and the Summary. Three independent lines of evidence: the printed text at 1393a and 1393d with no variant; Shrigondekar's own introduction naming "Dhoṣa" and "Iḍali" as the chapter's Kannada and Tamil survivals; and the secondary literature on this verse, which describes it as a paste of Bengal gram with asafoetida, cumin, and salt "spread on a hot oiled pan and cooked on both sides", with black gram as the second version. That description is 1392cd–1393 slot for slot.
- **V2. The *dhosaka* has no dough wrapper, and the reconstruction gave it one.** `वेष्टयित्वा गोलकेन` occurs only in the *veṣṭikā* clause. `तैलविलिप्तायां`, oil-smeared, occurs only in the *dhosaka* clause — and a dough parcel does not need an oiled griddle, whereas a bare pulse batter does. `विदलं` is singular neuter throughout, a mass; `धोसकान्` is plural, the resulting cakes. **Fixed:** the file is now split into two dishes with separate ingredients and methods, and the old note apologising that "the practical reconstructions overlap substantially" is gone, because they no longer do.
- **V3. `हरिमन्थ` is chickpea, and the note calling it uncertain was wrong.** It is a standard lexical synonym of `चणक` (*Cicer arietinum*) in Monier-Williams and the nighaṇṭus. The note had said "*Harimantha* is uncertain; mung or another small pulse is a reasonable choice." The reason the poet used the longer synonym is metrical: `हरिमन्थस्य विदलं` fills the pāda at eight where `चणकस्य विदलं` leaves it at seven. So both dishes use the same pulse and 1392's `विदलं चणकस्यैवं` is not introducing a second one — which is what makes the wrapper the real distinction.
- **V4. `पाकतत्त्ववित्` and `बुधः` dropped.** Both formulaic cook-epithets were absent. Restored, with `अनेनैव प्रकारेण` rendered as "by this very same method" rather than folded into "may also be made from".
- **V5. `राजमाष` was left transliterated in the translation but absent from the ingredients.** It is the cowpea or black-eyed pea, *Vigna unguiculata* — not the kidney bean, despite modern *rājmā*. Now offered in the *dhosaka* list alongside urad and dried peas. `वट्टाण` is the pea, *Pisum sativum*, the Prakrit form behind Marathi *vāṭāṇā*; the old "yellow split peas" was right and is kept.

### Judgement calls

- **V-J1. Boiled-and-mashed for the *veṣṭikā*, soaked-and-ground for the *dhosaka*.** Neither dish gets a boiling or grinding verb of its own; `पूर्वसम्भारसंस्कृतम्` reaches back only to 1391's seasonings, and the chapter supplies `उत्क्वाथ्य … पिष्ट्वा` two verses earlier for the *pūrikā*. The text therefore describes one seasoned pulse used two ways, and the split is mine: a raw ground filling will not cook through inside a bread in two minutes a side, and a boiled paste will not spread on a tawa. Flagged inline.

### Title

**Recommended, not applied: `Savoury *Veṣṭikā* and *Dhosaka*`.** I asterisked the loan words in the existing title but left "Ghosaka" standing, since renaming is Damon's call and the recommendation is prominent in the file's first note. If the `original` field is re-synced to `धोसकान्`, the title should follow.

## 8. *Kaṭakarṇa* Pulse Fritters (1394cd–1397ab)

### Confirmed

- **T1. The yeast and the 2–4 hour rise are gone.** See the Summary for the `kaṇḍu` verdict. The old note conceded that "*Kaṇḍu* is uncertain and may not be a ferment", but the ingredient list and method had already committed to the ferment anyway, which is the pattern the brief calls a reconstruction invention downstream of a translation gap.
- **T2. `चूर्णितं` means dry-milled, and the reconstruction soaked overnight and wet-ground.** `चूर्णित` is "reduced to powder" — the verb used of the millstones at 1376 and of every dry spice powder in the chapter — and `वारिणा सार्धं` then adds the water. `निष्पावचूर्ण` in the next line is unambiguously a dry flour. The secondary summary of this verse begins "chickpeas are powdered and mixed with salt, water, ghee, and powder of hyacinth bean." **Fixed:** the method now mills the pulses dry and slakes the flour, which also makes `पेषण्यां च प्रसारितम्` — rolled out on the grinding slab — a workable instruction, which it is not for a loose batter.
- **T3. `निष्पाव` is lablab, not fava.** *Lablab purpureus*, Indian *sem* / *val* / *avarai*. The chapter cooks it whole rather than split at 1365–66 and brines the pods at 1558. The note had said "Niṣpāva flour is represented here by fava-bean flour", and the ingredient list read "fava-bean flour". **Fixed** to *val* dal flour, with besan named as the fallback and the loss explained.
- **T4. The asafoetida is not in the text.** 1394cd–1396 gives ghee and rock salt and nothing else. The neighbours are seasoned in detail — hing, cumin, salt, and ginger for the *veṣṭikā*, hing, rock salt, sugar, pepper, and cardamom for the *pūrikā* — so the plainness here looks deliberate. Removed and noted.
- **T5. `सर्पिषा परिभावितम्` and `कटाहे तैलसंपूर्णे` were flattened.** *Pari-bhāvita* is "thoroughly permeated", the technical sense used in Ayurvedic *bhāvanā* for triturating a powder with a liquid until it takes it up; "condition with ghee" was not wrong but not informative. And `कटाहे तैलसंपूर्णे`, "in a cauldron brimming with oil", is the chapter's marked deep-fry, dropped entirely from the translation. Both restored, and the method now works the ghee in until absorbed.

### Judgement calls

- **T-J1. The dish is a thin cracker.** `पेषण्यां च प्रसारितम्` says spread out on the grinding slab and says nothing about thickness; the old reconstruction made 1 cm discs. A stiff dough rolled to 2–3 mm blisters, hardens, and matches `बुद्बुदसंकाशा … कनकत्विषः` far better than a thick disc, which browns outside before it cooks. Dimensions are mine. Flagged inline.

### Unresolved

- **T-U1. `कण्डु` itself.** Kept transliterated in the translation with a bracketed gloss, and labelled unresolved in the notes. The two readings — `कन्दु` an iron pan, or an instrument-noun from √*kaṇḍ* to pound — both give an implement, so the practical consequence is the same either way. The edition prints `कण्डुना` with no variant, so this is not a transmission problem to be solved by collation. If it is the pan, the step is plausibly a short cooking of the paste over heat, which the method now allows for.

Also flagged in the file: the 1397ab citation problem (§2) and the 1394c `वट्टाणकस्य` collation. `कटकर्ण` has `कर्ण`, "ear", as its second element and is probably a shape name like the *ghārikā*'s holes or the *cakkalikā*'s palmyra leaf — noted without a claim.

## 9. *Iḍerikā* Fermented Black-Gram Cakes (1397cd–1401ab)

The best of the seven before the audit, and still the one with the most restored.

### Confirmed

- **I1. Steamed, and the cloth is in the text.** See the Summary. The hedge is replaced by the argument, and the method now lines the moulds with damp muslin, which is what `वस्त्रगर्भाभिः` describes and what an idli plate is still lined with.
- **I2. `पेषण्यां`, `बहुशः`, and `तदहः` restored.** The grinding stone, the "many times over" of the beating, and the "for the day" of the rest were all compressed out. The beating matters: it is the aeration step, and it is the one instruction the verse repeats.
- **I3. The finishing order was wrong.** 1400 goes: off the fire → scatter powdered pepper → smeared with ghee → perfume with asafoetida-in-ghee and cumin. The method had merged the plain ghee smear into the tempering and put the pepper last. `घृताक्तान्` and `हिङ्गुसर्पिर्भ्यां … धूपयेत्` are two separate operations, matching the three-way distinction the *Mastu* notes already draw between `हिङ्गुतोय`, `हिङ्गुधूप`, and `हिङ्गुसर्पिस्`. Fixed.
- **I4. `वराः` dropped.** "These are the *excellent* *iḍerikās*." Restored, consistent with the *ghārikā* file's "the most delightful of *bhakṣyas*".
- **I5. `सुशीताः` is an instruction, not a description.** The cakes are served well cooled. The method said "Cool until the cakes are white and firm", which reads as a doneness test; it now says cool completely, and the intro says served cold. This is the detail most likely to surprise a cook who expects hot idlis.

### Judgement calls

- **I-J1. Hydration and timing.** The ingredient list read "120–180 ml water" as if that were the total, which would badly under-hydrate 250 g of dal. It now separates soaking water from grinding water (150–200 ml) and says the batter should hold a soft peak. 250 g of dry dal takes up roughly 350 ml during a 4–6 hour soak, so the finished batter runs about 2:1 water to dry pulse — thicker than idli batter, which is what lets the cakes hold shape in a shallow mould. Ferment narrowed from "8–16 hours in a warm place" to 8–14 at 28–30 °C, 16–20 somewhere cool, and the endpoint changed from "slightly sour and aerated" to actually sour, because `आम्लीभूतं` is the trigger for the next step. Numbers are mine.

### Unresolved

- **I-U1. `वैटिका`.** Printed as transcribed with no variant, so the manuscripts agree; the sense "small cups or bowls" is secure enough from the locative plus the covering vessels, and Marathi *vāṭī* is the likely descendant. But the secondary literature on this verse calls the vessels *piṭika* instead, and `पिटक` is a word this chapter does use for a vessel set over a pot's mouth, at 1356. Kept transliterated and labelled uncertain; nothing in the reconstruction turns on it.

Also noted: no rice anywhere in the batter (`माषपिष्ट` start to finish, confirmed by 1401's `तस्यैव माषपिष्टस्य`); `सम्भार` at 1398 is unspecified, so the salt and asafoetida in the batter are a reading of it rather than a translation; salt before fermenting slows the rise, and the text does put it before; and the 1397ab citation problem.

**On the downstream cluster:** the parent is now firm. `gharika-black-gram-rings.md` already reads "1 batch fermented iḍerikā batter" and has added `related: [iderika-fermented-black-gram-cakes, …]`, so the chain holds; I added the reciprocal link. The one thing the *ghārikā* agent should know is that the batter is now specified as genuinely sour rather than merely aerated, and that it is thicker than idli batter — both of which matter for shaping a ring with five or seven holes.
