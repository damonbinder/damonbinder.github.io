Two rustic breads made from the same enriched wheat dough as *maṇḍaka*: one thrown into embers, the other rolled out and cooked on a hot plate.

## Ingredients

* 1 batch *maṇḍaka* dough  
* Rice flour or wheat flour, for dusting  
* Ghee, for serving

## Method

1. For the ember breads, divide half the dough into 3 balls and flatten each between the palms into a thick round about 12 cm across.  
2. Rake out a bed of clean hardwood coals and brush the ash clear. Lay the rounds straight onto the coals, turning once, until they are lightly blackened in patches and cooked through — 3–5 minutes in all. Brush off any ash.  
3. Indoors, a baking stone or heavy tray heated to 250°C is the nearest substitute, though it will not blacken them: bake for 4–6 minutes, turning once.  
4. For griddle *polikās*, divide the remaining dough into 4 balls, dust generously, and roll to 10–12 cm rounds about 5 mm thick.  
5. Cook on a medium-hot griddle for 1–2 minutes per side, turning as soon as the underside has partly set, until mottled brown and cooked through. An earthen *kharpara* does the same job.  
6. Brush lightly with ghee and serve warm.

**Notes**

* The two breads differ in shaping and heat, not in dough. The *aṅgārapālikā* is flattened on the palm and thrown into the coals; the *polikā* is rolled out with a stone (*peṣaṇī*) and cooked on a heated plate — or on an earthen griddle, which the text offers as an alternative vessel rather than a second stage.  
* Slight blackening is the ember bread's mark of quality, the exact reverse of the *maṇḍaka*, which comes off the griddle before it colours at all. Breads cooked directly on coals survive in *bāṭī* and *liṭṭī*.  
* Burial in embers needs clean hardwood coals and careful ash removal. The hot-stone method is safer indoors but will not give the blackening the text asks for.  
* The transmitted text reads *aṅgārapālikā* at 1382 but *polikā* at 1383. *Aṅgārapolikā* is the form the secondary literature uses, and the one kept in the title here.  
* The term *polikā* is related to the later *poli* breads, but the recipe here does not specify a filling.
