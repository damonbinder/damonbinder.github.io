1543 is unusually careful about the animal: field-bred, riverbank-dwelling, large, dark, well-fed, and known by the vernacular name *mayiga*. Those are the diagnostic features of the greater bandicoot rat, *Bandicota indica*, which is still eaten in parts of India. The description is good enough that the substitution below is a matter of supply rather than of doubt about the species. Rabbit is close in size and in the colour of the meat.

## Ingredients

* 1 whole dressed rabbit, about 1.2 kg, cut into 8 pieces  
* 40 g tamarind concentrate  
* 300 ml water  
* 1½ tsp salt  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ½ tsp ground dried ginger  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* 20 g ghee or oil

## Method

1. Simmer the rabbit pieces with tamarind, water, and half the salt for 30–40 minutes, until nearly tender.  
2. Drain and pat dry. Thread manageable pieces onto skewers or arrange on a rack.  
3. Brush lightly with ghee — the source adds no fat, but a skinned rabbit needs it — and roast at 220°C or over charcoal until browned and dry at the edges.  
4. Mix the pepper, dried ginger, cumin, and remaining salt and scatter over the hot meat.

**Notes**

* The animal is de-haired, not skinned. 1544 scalds it and plucks the hairs out, the way the pig is de-bristled at 1427–1429, and 1546 confirms the skin is still on by making its drying the test for doneness: *yāvad bahis tvacas teṣāṃ śoṣam āyānti tāpanāt*. That is also why the source needs no basting fat, and why a skinned rabbit does.  
* *Pucchadhāraṇāt*: you hold the animal by the tail to dip it in the scalding water.  
* The sour cooking at 1545 is *pacet* with instrumental *amlaiḥ … lavaṇānvitaiḥ* — the plain verb "cook, boil", with the sours and the salt as the medium. It is a braise, and the salt belongs in the braising liquid; 1547 then scatters more salt over the finished meat. There is no marinade anywhere in this recipe.  
* The text spits the animals whole — *śūlapotān*, almost certainly for *śūlaprotān*, "transfixed on skewers". Jointing a rabbit into eight is a convenience.  
* Rabbit is an explicit substitution, not a zoological identification. Do not use wild rodents: leptospirosis, hantavirus, and plague are transmitted by handling rather than by eating, so cooking does not address them.
