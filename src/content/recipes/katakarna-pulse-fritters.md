A mixed-pulse fritter made from dry-milled pulse flour rather than a soaked batter, and neither soured nor leavened. The blistering the verse asks for is what a stiff, ghee-worked pulse dough does when it hits deep oil and its water flashes to steam. The dish is a thin, hard, bubbled cracker, closer to a *thattai* than to a *vada*.

## Ingredients

* 100 g yellow split peas, milled to a flour  
* 100 g split chickpeas, milled to a flour — or 100 g besan  
* 50 g lablab flour, milled from *val* dal — or a further 50 g besan; see notes  
* 150–180 ml water  
* 15 g ghee, melted  
* 1 tsp rock salt, finely ground  
* 700 ml neutral oil, for frying

## Method

1. If milling your own, grind the split peas and split chickpeas dry in a spice grinder and sift out anything coarse. Do the same with the *val* dal.  
2. Whisk the pea and chickpea flours with the water to a thick, smooth paste with no dry pockets, and leave it 15 minutes to hydrate fully.  
3. Work the melted ghee in a little at a time until the paste takes it all up and turns glossy, then stir in the rock salt.  
4. Beat hard for 3–4 minutes with a wooden spoon, or in a heavy pan over the lowest heat until the paste thickens and comes away from the sides.  
5. Knead in the lablab flour to give a stiff, rollable dough. Rest it 10 minutes.  
6. Roll out thin, 2–3 mm, on an oiled stone slab or board, and cut into pieces about 6 cm across.  
7. Fry at 180 °C until blistered, hard, and golden, turning once — 2–3 minutes. Drain on a rack; they crisp as they cool.

**Notes**

* **There is no leaven in this recipe, and there never was.** An earlier version of this reconstruction added instant yeast or sourdough starter and a 2–4 hour rise on the strength of `कण्डु`. Nothing in 1394–97 sours or rests the mixture. The contrast with the *iḍerikā* three verses later is the point: there the text says `स्थापयेत्तदहः`, leave it for the day, and `आम्लीभूतं`, once it has turned sour. Here it says neither. And a dough made from dry flour and water has no soaking time in which a wild population could establish itself.  
* **What `कण्डु` is.** An implement, on either of the two live readings. `कन्दु` is attested in Suśruta as an iron pot or boiler, gives `कान्दव` "baked in an iron pan" and `कान्दविक` "a baker", and survives as Kannada *kaṃdu*, a frying pan. Alternatively the retroflex form points at √*kaṇḍ*, to husk or pound, which gives `कण्डन` "pounding" and `कण्डनी` "a wooden mortar". Either way `परिघट्टितम्` is "stirred, worked about" — the same construction as `दर्व्या परिघट्टयेत्` at 1354, an implement in the instrumental. Shrigondekar prints `कण्डुना` with no variant. If it is the pan, the step is plausibly a brief cooking of the paste over heat, which is what step 4 allows for. [Unresolved: the word is not securely identified, and it is kept transliterated for that reason. What is secure is that it cannot be a starter.]  
* **The pulses are milled dry, then slaked.** `चूर्णितं` is "reduced to powder", the same verb used of the millstones at 1376; you cannot powder a soaked pulse. `वारिणा सार्धं` then adds the water. So this is a besan-style dough, not a wet-ground batter — which is also what makes `पेषण्यां च प्रसारितम्`, rolled out on the grinding slab, a possible instruction.  
* **`निष्पाव` is the lablab or hyacinth bean**, *Lablab purpureus*, Indian *sem* / *val* / *avarai* — not the fava bean, which is an unrelated plant. The chapter cooks `निष्पाव` whole rather than split at 1365–66 and brines the pods at 1558. Mill *val* dal from an Indian grocer; besan is the fallback but loses the point of the third flour, which is that it goes in last, dry, to stiffen the dough.  
* **No asafoetida.** The passage gives this dish ghee and rock salt and nothing else — worth honouring, because the neighbours are seasoned in detail: hing, cumin, salt, and ginger for the *veṣṭikā* at 1391, hing, rock salt, sugar, pepper, and cardamom for the *pūrikā* at 1389. An earlier version of the ingredient list carried a pinch of asafoetida that the text does not ask for.  
* `कटकर्ण` has `कर्ण`, "ear", as its second element and is probably a shape name, like the *ghārikā*'s holes or the *cakkalikā*'s palmyra leaf. The chapter gives no thickness or diameter, so the cracker dimensions above are a choice.  
* **A citation problem.** The `यावद्` clause that finishes this recipe — `यावद्बुद्बुदसंकाशा भवन्ति कनकत्विषः`, "until they come out like bubbles, with the sheen of gold" — is 1397a–b, and it sits in the *iḍerikā* entry's `original` rather than this one. The citation should read 1394–1397a. Also collated: 1394c reads `वट्टाणकस्य` in the printed edition, not `वट्टाणस्य` as transcribed above.
