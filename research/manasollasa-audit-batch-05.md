# Mānasollāsa 3.13.1453–1475 — audit of five recipes (batch 05)

*Created 2026-07-25 12:08.*

Scope: `kavacandi-mixed-fry.md` (1453–56), `puryala-covered-meat.md` (1457–61, **published**), `bhaditraka-skewers.md` (1462–65, **published**), `sauced-bhaditraka.md` (1466–68, **published**), `krsnapaka-blood-finished-mutton.md` (1469–75). All five were edited. No other file in `src/` was touched; `src/data/sources/manasollasa.ts` was read only.

## Summary

**Four of the five had substantive errors. Puryala is clean** — it is the best-translated Mānasollāsa entry I have seen, carrying every element of five verses including the *pūlikā* coriander bundle and the `वरम्` epithet, and its chicken adaptation rests on a claim that turns out to be exactly right.

**The most consequential correction is in the kavacandī.** `पलाण्ड्वार्द्रकसम्भूतान् मुद्गाङ्कुरविनिर्मितान्` (1454c–d) are accusative plural participles with no head noun of their own, and the only head available is `वटकान्` at the start of the next line. **The onion, the ginger, and the mung sprouts are what the fritters are made of — they are not separate things thrown in the pan.** The translation listed all three as loose ingredients and the reconstruction fried onion with the vegetables and folded raw sprouts in at the end, so a dish with one composite element in it had been flattened into a dish with four scattered ones. Second in that recipe: `मेषक` is almost certainly `मेथक`, fenugreek — the identical compound `मेथकचूर्णकं` appears four verses later at 1459 and `मेथकस्य च` at 1467 — and the reconstruction was standing chopped herbs in for it. Third: `कासमर्द` was labelled an "uncertain plant term" when it is securely *Senna occidentalis*, whose leaves are eaten as a pot-herb in coastal Karnataka, which is precisely this text's region.

**Before cooking anything here, the thing to know is in the kṛṣṇapāka.** Its method added 200 ml of cold blood straight into a hot, strongly acidic pot and then simmered it for 8–10 minutes with no temperature target. Acid lowers the temperature at which blood proteins aggregate, so that sequence reliably granulates, and "cook gently until thickened" is not a safety criterion — blood sets a good 20 °C below the temperature at which it is safe to eat. The method now reduces the sour liquid first, tempers the blood with hot cooking liquor, and names 71 °C on a probe with an explicit ceiling below a simmer. The source's own two blood steps turn out to be functional: the pinch of salt at 1469 retards clotting and kneading out the `सिराजाल` removes the fibrin that would otherwise show as strings.

**`मूषकैः` (1462d): unresolved, and I do not think it is resolvable from the transmitted text.** The syntax is not in doubt — instrumental plural running into `विध्वा`, so it is what the meat is pierced with — but no attested sense of `मूषक` is a tool (rat, mouse, thief, crucible, a leech, a metre, a people, and *Solanum nigrum*), and this chapter's own `मूषक` at 1543–47 is a rodent you eat. The pāda scans clean at 8, and any `-कैः` instrumental fits the slot, which is exactly the shape of word that goes astray in transmission. It is now translated as `*mūṣakas*` with a bracketed note rather than dropped.

**The bird claim in Puryala's intro is true, and I found the verse.** 3.13.1522–23: `पक्षिणामपि सर्वेषां … पूर्ववत्परिपाचयेत् । यथा सूकरमेषाणां क्रियाः प्रोक्ता विपाचने` — clean birds, then "cook as before, just as the operations for pig and sheep have been stated." The chapter also rates fowl in its meat survey (`शाकुनं लघु कीर्तितम्`, 1419b) and gives no bird recipe. Both halves of the intro's assertion check out and the citation is now on the page.

## 1. Kavacandī (1453–1456) — `kavacandi-mixed-fry.md`, draft

### Confirmed

**K1. The onion, ginger, and mung sprouts belong to the *vaṭakas*.** `वार्ताकशकलांश्चैव मूलकस्य च खण्डकान् । पलाण्ड्वार्द्रकसम्भूतान् मुद्गाङ्कुरविनिर्मितान् ॥ वटकान् निक्षिपेत्तत्र` — the first two items are their own heads (`शकलान्`, `खण्डकान्`), but `सम्भूतान्` and `विनिर्मितान्` are participles that need one, and the only accusative plural masculine noun available is `वटकान्` across the verse boundary. So: throw in there eggplant slices, radish pieces, and *vaṭakas* made from mung sprouts with onion and fresh ginger. The old translation ("Add eggplant, radish, onion, ginger, mung sprouts, *vaṭakas*, …") reads the modifiers as siblings of the things they modify. Two sub-readings are possible — one kind of *vaṭaka* described twice over, or two kinds (onion-ginger and mung-sprout) — and the absence of a connective between the two participles plus the fact that sprouted mung binds itself while an onion fritter would need a binder the text does not name both favour the single-item reading. Either way the three ingredients are fritter material. Fixed in translation, ingredients (a `For the *vaṭakas*` sub-list), and method (a new step 1).

**K2. `कासमर्द` is not an uncertain plant term.** It is *Senna occidentalis* (formerly *Cassia occidentalis*), coffee senna / *kasondi* — the Ayurvedic drug `कासमर्द`, "cough-crusher" — and its leaves are eaten as a vegetable in South Indian households, including a *tambuḷi* in coastal Karnataka. That is the *Mānasollāsa*'s own region. The old note called it uncertain and omitted; the new note identifies it and says a bitter green stands in because the plant isn't sold outside India.

**K3. `मेषक` is the uncertain one, and it was silently replaced by "fresh herbs".** See the judgement call J-K1 for the resolution; the confirmed part is that the notes had the uncertainty assigned to the wrong word of the two — the same defect the chickpea audit found in a neighbouring recipe.

**K4. Two of the `पूर्ववत्` powders had been dropped.** `पूर्ववच्चूर्णमिश्रितान्` back-references 1449b–1450a: `निशाजीरकतीक्ष्णाद्यैः शुण्ठीधान्यकहिङ्गुभिः` — turmeric, cumin, pepper-and-the-like, dried ginger, coriander, asafoetida. The ingredient list had turmeric, cumin, coriander, and pepper; **dried ginger and asafoetida had vanished into the back-reference.** Both added.

**K5. The powders go on the meat before it is fried.** In 1453 `चूर्णमिश्रितान्` is an attribute of the `खण्डान्` at the moment they enter the oil, exactly as at 1449 (`चूर्णितैर्मेलयित्वा तांस्तप्ततैले विनिक्षिपेत्`). The old method added the spices at step 3, after the vegetables were already softening. Resequenced.

**K6. The name is not explained by the text, and `शुभा` had been converted into an etymology.** 1456d is `नानाद्रव्यसमेता सा कवचन्दी भवेच्छुभा` — "provided with many substances, that *kavacandī* is a good one". The old translation had "Because it contains many substances, it is called *kavacandī*", and the old note asserted "The name is explained by the source itself as referring to the dish's many ingredients." It isn't; the text characterizes the dish, and `शुभा` is a praise word, not a gloss. Both fixed.

Also fixed in passing: `बदराकारकान्` is a *shape* word (`आकार`) where the ginger's `तत्प्रमाणान्` is a *measure* word, and the ginger's cut size — "of that same measure", i.e. 2 cm, the same as the meat — had become "cut into small pieces"; `पलान्यन्यानि कानिचित्` ("some other meats") had become "any other suitable meats **or vegetables**", which imports a licence the text doesn't give, and is now an optional second meat in the ingredient list; `कासमर्देन संयुक्तं` agrees with `चूर्णकम्`, so the *kāsamarda* goes in *with* the powder, which the old list-style rendering lost; and `सुसिद्धं वासयेद्धूपैः` — the fumigation — was missing from the method entirely and is now the final step.

### Judgement calls

**J-K1. `मेषकस्य च चूर्णकम्` (1455b) is very probably `मेथकस्य च चूर्णकम्`, fenugreek powder. Applied.** ½ tsp ground fenugreek is now in the ingredients; the translation keeps `*meṣaka*` with a bracket. Grounds, in order of force:

- `मेथकचूर्णकं` occurs at **1459a**, four verses later, as the identical compound with the identical head.
- `मेथकस्य च` occurs at **1467d**, as the identical genitive-plus-*ca*. `मेथकस्य च चूर्णकम्` is those two attested halves joined, and scans identically to the transmitted line.
- **`चूर्णक` means a powdered spice every time it occurs in this chapter** — 1459 (fenugreek), 1475 (camphor), 1477 (asafoetida and rock salt), and here. Where the chapter means minced meat it says `चूर्णीकृतं मांसं` (1479), `चूर्णितं मांसं` (1482), `चूर्णमांसेन` (1485). That kills the otherwise tempting reading `मेष` = sheep → mutton mince, which `पलान्यन्यानि` ("some *other* meats") two pādas later would otherwise support.
- No spice or plant named `मेषक` is attested. The nearest is `मेषशृङ्गी` (*Gymnema sylvestre*, in this chapter's pickling list at 1554), whose leaves are aggressively bitter and implausible as a powder here.
- The competing conjecture `माषकस्य` (urad flour) needs the same single-character slip and has no support in the neighbourhood; the *vaṭakas* here are mung, not urad.

I put this at roughly 70%. Revert by deleting the fenugreek line and the bracket.

**J-K2. `नानारसविमिश्रितम्` (1456b) as the sour sauce of 1442–48.** The reconstruction's 150 ml of sour meat glaze is retained, but the note now says this is an inference: 1452 reuses that sauce with an explicit `पूर्वोदितं`, and 1453–56 gives no such pointer, so "mixed with the various juices" is being read in the light of the neighbour rather than on its own authority. `रस` could also just mean "flavours".

**J-K3. Oil raised from 30 ml to 45 ml, matching the chickpea audit's figure for the same construction.** `पक्वतैले विपाचयेत्` is a shallow-fry, and 30 ml will not fry 400 g of meat plus 300 g of vegetables. Also: the 1½ tsp salt is retained but flagged — **no salt appears anywhere in 1453–1456**, and if the sauce is the 1442–48 one it arrives carrying its own rock salt.

### Unresolved

`मेषक`, if J-K1 is rejected. Nothing else in the passage is philologically open: `बदर` (jujube), `वार्ताक` (eggplant), `मूलक` (radish), `मुद्गाङ्कुर` (mung sprout), `वटक` (fried pulse cake), `पलाण्डु` (onion), and `कासमर्द` are all secure.

## 2. Puryala (1457–1461) — `puryala-covered-meat.md`, published

**No errors. The translation carries all twenty pādas.** I checked element by element: `स्थूलामलक` size comparison, `शुद्ध`, `काथयेत्`, `राजिकातोयैः`, both gingers from `नागरार्द्रक`, `पात्रे रिक्ते`, `अम्लैः`, `तत्समान् शुण्ठकान्`, `सैन्धवं`, `मेथकचूर्णकं`, `धान्यकस्य पूलिकाम्` (a *bundle* of coriander — a nice catch that most translations would smooth away), `उत्तारयेत्सूदो`, `घृतं वान्यत्र तापयेत्`, `सुतप्ते घृते`, `पलाण्डुं लशुनं हिङ्गुना सह`, `संस्कृतं मांसं`, `तस्यां स्थाल्यां प्रवेशयेत्`, `पिहितं`, `किंचित्कालं प्रतीक्ष्य`, and the `वरम्` epithet. Nothing dropped, nothing generalized, nothing mis-anchored.

### The bird question — confirmed, with the verse

The intro asserts two things and both are true.

- **"Contains no poultry dishes."** True. There is no bird recipe anywhere in 3.13. The chapter runs pig → sheep and other quadrupeds → birds (cleaning only) → fish → turtle → crab → *mūṣaka* → vegetables, and the bird entry is two verses of butchery with no dish attached.
- **"It advised that birds can be prepared in exactly the same manner as meat."** True, at **3.13.1522–23**: `पक्षिणामपि सर्वेषां पिच्छानुत्सार्य सर्वतः । चञ्चुपादौ पृथक्कृत्वा पाटयित्वा ततोदरम् ॥ निष्कृष्यान्त्रादिकं सर्वं पूर्ववत्परिपाचयेत् । यथा सूकरमेषाणां क्रियाः प्रोक्ता विपाचने ॥` — "Of all birds too, having removed the feathers all round, separated the beak and feet, and split open the belly, having drawn out the entrails and all the rest, one should cook them **as before, just as the operations for pigs and sheep have been stated** in cooking." That is about as explicit a licence as the text could give. Corroborating: 1419b rates fowl-meat in the dietary survey (`शाकुनं लघु कीर्तितम्`, "bird meat is declared light"), so birds are inside the chapter's meat taxonomy and simply have no recipe of their own.

The citation is now on the page as a `## Notes` bullet. I did **not** touch the intro prose.

### Judgement calls

**J-P1. Whole drumsticks vs the source's cut.** `शुद्धमांसस्य खण्डकान्` the size of large *āmalakas* is boneless trimmed meat at 25–35 mm. Drumsticks are bone-in and skin-on. Damon cooked this and it is a household favourite, so per the standing rule the method is untouched and the divergence is noted instead.

**J-P2. `घृतं वान्यत्र तापयेत्` (1459d).** The `वा` could make the whole ghee-and-aromatics finish an *alternative* to simply taking the pot off the fire, rather than the next step. I read it as connective — the dish is only named `पुर्यल` after the covered ghee step, which is its climax — but a stricter reading of `वा` would make 1457–59 a complete dish and 1460–61 an optional enrichment. Not acted on.

Minor and not acted on: the translation has "salt" where the text has `सैन्धव`, rock salt; and `अम्लैः` is plural, where the reconstruction uses tamarind alone (legitimate under the `द्वित्रान्` licence of 1443, which permits one).

## 3. Bhaḍitraka skewers (1462–1465) — `bhaditraka-skewers.md`, published

**Today's size note and the 4 cm quantity are preserved verbatim and untouched. I found corroboration for the note rather than against it** — see B3 — and appended one clause to it.

### Confirmed

**B1. The 1465 praise epithets were dropped.** `नाम्ना भडित्रकं रुच्यं लघु पथ्यं मनोहरम्` — four epithets, all gone from "This preparation is called *bhaditraka*." The parallel published Puryala carries its `वरम्`, so this was an inconsistency as well as an omission. Restored as "By name this is *bhaditraka* — tasty, light, wholesome, and delightful."

**B2. `मूषकैः` restored to the translation** with a bracketed note instead of silent deletion, and `बहुरन्ध्राणि कारयेत्` ("make them full of holes") made explicit alongside `विध्वा बहुशः`.

### Judgement call

**J-B1. `पेषयेत्` is "grind", not "rub". Applied.** `हिङ्ग्वार्द्रकरसैर्युक्तं सैन्धवेन च पेषयेत्` — the neuter singular `युक्तं` agrees with `मांसं` back in 1462b, so it is the *meat* that is worked, furnished with the juices and the salt. "Rub them with" became "Work the juices of asafoetida and fresh ginger, and rock salt, into them." Same edit fixed `हिङ्ग्वार्द्रकरसैः` — a single dvandva, "juices of asafoetida and fresh ginger" — which had been split into "asafoetida-water, ginger juice" (`हिङ्गुतोय` is the chapter's asafoetida-water, at 1478 and 1513, and it is not the word here). The ingredient list already dissolves asafoetida in water, so nothing changes for the cook.

### Unresolved — `मूषकैः` (1462d)

The full case, since this was the standing open item.

**The syntax is settled.** `घनसारप्रमाणानि कृत्वा खण्डानि मूषकैः ॥ विध्वा तु बहुशस्तानि बहुरन्ध्राणि कारयेत्` — the sentence needs an instrument for `विध्वा`, the seasonings are given separately in 1463c–d, and there is no other candidate. It is a piercing tool.

**The lexica have no tool.** `मूषक` / `मूष` / `मूषिका`: mouse or rat; thief or plunderer; a crucible (= `मूषा`, Böhtlingk–Roth); a species of leech; a metre; a people and a river in Andhra; and in one botanical database *Solanum nigrum*. Nothing pointed, nothing hand-held.

**The rodent reading is dead.** This chapter's own `मूषक` at 1543–47 is the field-or-riverbank animal that gets scalded, skinned, and skewered, i.e. food. Animals do not pierce, and the syntax will not let this be an ingredient.

**Every conjecture with the right sense is graphically remote from the transmitted word** — `सूचकैः` (needles), `कीलकैः` (pins), `शङ्कुभिः` (spikes), `शूलकैः` (little spits, and `शूल` is the passage's own skewer word at 1464a). All fit the metre; none is a plausible misreading of `मूषकैः` in Devanagari. A Dravidian route is conceivable — the chapter is full of Kannada-region food vocabulary (`चक्कलिका`, `कवचन्दी`, `भडित्रक`, `पुर्यल`, `मयिग`, `कोशली`) and Kannada *muḷḷu* / Tamil *muḷ* is a thorn or spike, which would give a Sanskritized `*मुल्लकैः` of the right shape and meaning — but the manuscripts behind the printed edition are North Indian Devanagari copies, which makes a ḷ/ṣ script confusion hard to route. I would not put this above a guess.

**Two facts about the transmission are worth stating.** The pāda scans clean at 8, so this is not an obvious interpolation. But it is a pāda-final three-syllable instrumental plural, and *any* `-कैः` form fits the slot exactly, which is the single easiest kind of word to lose. And Shrigondekar's own introduction to the volume containing this chapter says his four manuscripts are: A, a copy made from three others; B, incomplete; D, which "did not give much help … since the subjects treated of in this volume are unusual; it is full of mistakes"; and F, "complete but full of mistakes". A corrupt technical implement-word in a butchery passage is exactly what that manuscript base predicts. **Verdict: UNRESOLVED. Collate 1462 against the GOS text and its apparatus; do not emend.**

## 4. Sauced bhaḍitraka (1466–1468) — `sauced-bhaditraka.md`, published

### Confirmed

**S1. `अनेनैव प्रकारेण` was dropped, and it is the most informative phrase in the passage.** "In this very same way" — a back-reference to the whole preceding preparation. Combined with `जर्जरीकृत्य`, "having made them *jarjara*" (worn, battered, riddled), it points straight at 1463's `विध्वा तु बहुशस्तानि बहुरन्ध्राणि कारयेत्`. So the pieces here are the loin pieces of 1462, cut camphor-lump-sized and pierced full of holes, and only the cooking differs. The old note hedged this as "The text **may** describe the same cut"; the text says so.

**S2. `जर्जरीकृत्य` is piercing, not scoring or shredding.** "Roughen or shred meat pieces" generalizes a specific technique that the sister recipe spells out two verses earlier. Restored as "riddle the pieces with holes".

**S3. The dish keeps its name, and the name was dropped.** 1468d: `क्षिपेच्च मरिचं भृष्टे सूदो भण्डे भडित्रके` — "and the cook should throw pepper on the *bhaḍitraka* once it has fried in the pan." Both `भण्डे` (the pan) and `भडित्रके` (the dish name) were missing. Restored. Note for the record: the text's own description of this dish is "*bhaḍitraka* fried in a pan", which would make **Pan-Fried *Bhaḍitraka*** a more source-anchored title than "Sauced Bhaditraka" — flagging, not changing, since the title is Damon's.

Also restored: `स्थाल्याम्` (in a pot), `संयोज्य` (joined with), the two separate `रस` genitives at 1467, and `घृतेन परिभोजयेत्` — literally "feed it with ghee", which is more generous than "finish with ghee".

### Judgement calls

**J-S1. Method step 1 changed from scoring/pounding to piercing. Applied, flagged.** Before: "Score or lightly pound the meat pieces to roughen their surfaces." After: "Pierce the meat pieces repeatedly with a skewer or fork, as for the skewered *bhaditraka*." Loin does not want to be braised dry and then fried, and the recipe is published on the strength of having been cooked with "lamb or pork" unspecified, so per the standing rule the ingredient line is untouched and a note records the divergence.

Also noted rather than changed: **no salt is named in 1466–1468.** Either it carries over from the rock salt worked into the pieces at 1463 — the same back-reference that S1 establishes — or it is assumed. Since 1466d and 1467 *re-list* the asafoetida and the ginger juice that 1463 already had, the back-reference probably covers the cutting and piercing rather than the whole rub, which leaves the salt unlicensed. Either way the 1½ tsp is the reconstruction's and now says so.

## 5. Kṛṣṇapāka (1469–1475) — `krsnapaka-blood-finished-mutton.md`, draft

Seven verses, of which three and a half had been compressed to nothing. This is the same defect the Śuṇṭhaka audit found: a butchery passage summarized instead of translated.

### Confirmed

**C1. The blood handling loses its technique.** `मर्दयेत्करशाखाभिः` (1469c) — "he should knead it with the branches of the hand", i.e. work it with the fingers — had become "stir it", with `करशाखाभिः` gone. The instrument matters: you work the blood by hand precisely so the fibrin gathers on your fingers and can be lifted out, which is the point of 1470's `सिराजालविनिःसृते`. And `पिहितं न्यसेत्` (1470d) — "set it down **covered**" — had been dropped entirely. Both restored; the method's step 1 now works the blood by hand or whisk before straining.

**C2. The throat-cut is dropped.** `मेषस्य कन्धरां छित्त्वा` (1469a) — "having cut the sheep's neck" — became "Catch sheep's blood in a pot", which loses the instruction and with it the reason the blood is fresh. Restored to the translation; deliberately not reconstructed.

**C3. All of 1471 — the skinning — is missing.** `क्रोडदेशात्समारभ्य कर्तरिकाग्रेण पाटिताम् । त्वचं विभज्य गात्रेभ्यो हरेदाभ्यन्तरास्थिताम्` — begin at the chest, split the hide with the point of a knife, separate it from the limbs, and take away the inner-lying skin. Restored. (`क्रोडदेश` is the chest region, as at 1507, not the boar sense of `क्रोड` that the same chapter uses at 1512.)

**C4. Two elements of 1472 are missing, one of them a back-reference.** `पूर्वोद्दिष्टप्रदेशेषु स्थितं मांसम्` — "the meat lying in the regions indicated earlier" — points at the anatomical survey of 1417–26, which is where `पृष्ठवंश` and the rest of this chapter's cut names are set out. And `स्नायुग्रन्थिविनिर्मुक्तं`, "freed from sinew and knots", had been compressed into the single word "cleaned". Both restored.

**C5. `पूर्ववत्` at 1473 occurs twice and only one was carried.** `पूगीफलप्रमाणानि कृत्वा खण्डानि पूर्ववत् । संस्कुर्यात्पूर्ववच्चूर्णैः` — the cutting is "as before" as well as the seasoning. Restored. **`पूगीफलप्रमाणानि` itself is present and correct** — the chapter-wide size sweep did carry it, and 2 cm is right for an areca nut. Verified, not re-fixed.

**C6. `वरम्` was dropped.** 1475d is `कृष्णपाकमिदं वरम्`, and Puryala's identically-shaped `पुर्यलाख्यमिदं वरम्` is rendered "This **excellent** preparation is called *puryala*". Restored for consistency.

Also: the finish had lost a step. `धूपयेद्धिङ्गुजीरकैः` (1474d) is a *separate* asafoetida-and-cumin fumigation, distinct from the asafoetida in the 1449 powder list, and the reconstruction had folded it away — its step 5 poured in plain ghee and scattered the dry spices. The method now blooms asafoetida and cumin seeds in the ghee (this collection's standing stand-in for `धूप`) and keeps 1475's dry scatter of camphor, cardamom, and pepper as the later, separate step it is.

### Will it work? Blood, acid, and heat

**As written it would have granulated, and it had no safety criterion.** Three problems, all now addressed in the method:

1. **Cold blood into a hot acidic pot.** Blood proteins begin setting around 65–70 °C and go grainy above roughly 80 °C, and acid lowers the temperature at which they aggregate — the same mechanism that makes the sour curd glaze of 1442–48 fragile. 200 ml of tamarind water plus 100 ml of pomegranate juice is a lot of acid to drop cold blood into. Fix: reduce the sour liquid first, then temper the blood with a ladle of hot cooking liquor before it goes back in. Filipino *dinuguan* and Polish *czernina* both use acid with blood successfully, and both do it in this order.
2. **"Cook gently until thickened" is not a safety test.** Blood thickens well below a safe temperature, so thickening tells you nothing. Fix: 71 °C / 160 °F on a probe, with an explicit ceiling — hold it at a bare tremble, because a boil granulates. The window between safe and ruined is real but wide enough to hit with a thermometer.
3. **Sourcing and handling.** Blood is an excellent bacterial medium and degrades fast. The old note said "Use only blood sold for culinary use and keep it cold" — true but thin. The note now specifies a licensed butcher or abattoir, ≤4 °C, use within a day or two, never taste raw, serve immediately, don't repeatedly reheat, and flags that retail sale for eating is restricted or unusual in some countries including much of the United States.

**The source's own protocol is competent, and worth saying so.** The pinch of salt at 1469 retards clotting; kneading out the `सिराजाल` removes the fibrin that would otherwise appear as strings in the finished sauce; and covering the defibrinated `कीलाल` keeps it clean while the butchery proceeds. That is three of the four things a modern blood-sauce recipe tells you to do.

### Judgement calls

**J-C1. Reducing before the blood goes in. Applied, flagged.** `स्तोकावशेषपाके` means "when only a little of the cooking remains" — it is about *time*, not liquid, and the old translation's "When almost done" was right. But the blood has to have something to disperse into that isn't a soup, and the sister recipe at 1468 dries its pot out entirely (`शोषयित्वा द्रवं सर्वं`), so reducing to a coating is the reading that cooks. The note says explicitly that this is inferred.

**J-C2. Salt.** The only salt in the passage is `लवणं मनाक्`, the pinch in the blood, and it is functional rather than seasoning; the 1449 powder list has none either. The 1½ tsp is retained and labelled a modern addition.

### Unresolved

`हरेदाभ्यन्तरास्थिताम्` (1471d). `आभ्यन्तरा-स्थिताम्` is feminine accusative agreeing with `त्वचम्`, so "the skin situated within" — plausibly the inner membrane or fell taken off after the hide. The half-verse above it is one syllable over metre, so the wording here may have drifted. Translated plainly as "the inner-lying skin" and flagged in the notes as uncertain.

## 7. Transcription integrity — metrical scan

Scanned by half-verse (16 syllables) rather than by pāda, because sandhi crosses pāda boundaries in this text — `शूलपोतानि कृत्वा तान्यङ्गारेषु प्रतापयेत्` looks like 7+9 by pāda and is a clean 16 by half-verse.

| Passage | Half-verses | Clean | Pādas | Defects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1453–1456 kavacandī | 8 | **8/8** | 16/16 | — |
| 1457–1461 puryala | 10 | 9/10 | 18/20 | 1460a–b at **17** |
| 1462–1465 bhaḍitraka | 8 | **8/8** | 16/16 | — |
| 1466–1468 sauced bhaḍitraka | 6 | **6/6** | 12/12 | — |
| 1469–1475 kṛṣṇapāka | 14 | 13/14 | 26/28 | 1471a–b at **17** |
| **Total** | 46 | **44/46** | **92/96** | 2 |

For comparison with the prior audits: 1442–48 scored 28/28 pādas, 1449–52 16/16, and 1427–35 only 28/36. This batch sits with the clean passages.

The two defects are both **one syllable over**, which is the mildest kind:

- **1460a `सुतप्ते च घृते पलाण्डुं`** (9). Dropping either `सु-` or `च` restores 8 exactly: `तप्ते च घृते पलाण्डुं` or `सुतप्ते घृते पलाण्डुं`. Sense is unaffected — very hot ghee either way. Looks like an intrusive syllable rather than a lost reading.
- **1471b `कर्तरिकाग्रेण पाटिताम्`** (9). A compound would fix it: `कर्तरिकाग्रपाटिताम्` = 8, or `कर्तर्याग्रेण पाटिताम्` = 8. Note the pattern: **the secondary form `कर्तरिका` appears in defective pādas in both places it occurs** — here, and at 1432b `कर्तरिकापरिपाटितम्` (10 syllables, in the passage the Śuṇṭhaka audit found corrupt) — while the plain `कर्तर्या` at 1476b scans clean. That is weak evidence that the `-इका-` extension is a transmission artifact rather than the author's word.

**Recommendation: collate 1460 and 1471 against Shrigondekar's GOS text, along with 1462 for `मूषकैः`.** I did not emend any Devanagari. Worth knowing before doing so: the GOS volume containing this chapter is edited from four manuscripts, and Shrigondekar's own introduction describes B as incomplete, D as having "did not give much help in the preparation of this volume, since the subjects treated of in this volume are unusual; it is full of mistakes", and F as "complete but full of mistakes". The scanned volume is on archive.org (`in.ernet.dli.2015.280843`) but only the English introduction was OCR'd — the Sanskrit body text is images, so the verses have to be read off the page scans.

## 9. What would falsify the main findings

- **K1 (the *vaṭaka* head noun)** would fall if the GOS text reads `वटकैः` or breaks the verse differently, or if either participle turns out to be `-आन्` for a different stem. The reading rests on the participles having no other possible head across the verse boundary, which is a strong argument but a purely internal one.
- **J-K1 (`मेषक` = fenugreek)** would fall to a GOS reading of `मेषकस्य` with an apparatus note, or to any attestation of `मेषक` as a spice. It would be strengthened by a variant `मेथकस्य` in any manuscript.
- **`मूषकैः`** turns on the apparatus. If all four manuscripts agree on `मूषकैः`, the corruption is older than this transmission and the word may be a genuine regional term with no lexical footprint — in which case the honest position stays where it is.
- **The bird licence at 1522–23** is not in doubt; the text is explicit and the corroboration at 1419 is independent.
