Whole vegetables are hollowed, packed with the crumbled meat of the preceding recipe, browned briefly in oil, and then cooked through under cover in something savoury.

## Ingredients

* 4 small eggplants, or 2 small ridge gourds, or 4 large daikon sections  
* 300–400 g minced meat, seasoned as in the *Māṃsa-Vaṭaka* recipe — the crumbly *cūrṇa* meat, not the sticky paste  
* 30 ml oil  
* 150 ml light stock  
* ½ tsp salt

## Method

1. Bore a hole at the stem end of each vegetable and work out the seed core, keeping the walls intact. The text makes a hole rather than cutting a lid, so there is nothing to pin back on.  
2. Pack the crumbled meat in firmly.  
3. Heat the oil in a pan and brown the vegetables briefly on several sides — *tailena pācitaṃ kiṃcit*, "cooked a little in oil".  
4. Add the stock and salt, cover, and cook gently until both vegetable and filling are done and the filling reads 71°C: 25–35 minutes for eggplant, 30–40 for parboiled daikon, and 12–18 for ridge gourd, which collapses if you give it the eggplant's time.

**Notes**

* For daikon, parboil the hollowed sections for 5 minutes before filling.  
* **The dish name means "stuffed eggplant".** *Bhaṭṭāka* stands for *bhaṇṭāka*/*bhaṇṭakī*, which the Bhāvaprakāśa lists among the synonyms of *vārtāka*, the eggplant — the same word as Hindi *bhaṇṭā*, and the one behind English "brinjal". So *pūra-bhaṭṭāka* is "filled eggplant", and 1485d's rule that each version takes "the name of its own vegetable" makes a stuffed *kośātakī* a *pūra-kośātakī* and a stuffed radish a *pūra-mūlaka*.
* *Kośātakī* is most often identified as Luffa acutangula, the ridged or angled luffa (Hindi *torai*), which is what this uses. The lexica also allow Luffa cylindrica and Trichosanthes dioica, the pointed gourd — and pointed gourd is the vegetable Indian cooks actually stuff with spiced mince today, so it is worth trying if ridge gourd is hard to find.
* **The filling is the crumb, not the paste.** 1483d fills the eggplant with *tena māṃsena*, "that meat", picking up the preceding recipe, and 1485c names it *cūrṇamāṃsena*. That is the drier of the two meat preparations at 1479–80 — the one the paste balls were wrapped around, rather than the paste itself.
* *Svādunā paripācayet* (1484d), "cook it through with something savoury", is a separate instruction, and it is the reason for the stock: the covered stage gets a flavored liquid rather than water. *Svādu* is only "tasty, savoury", so what goes in is left to the cook. This is also the least reliable line in the passage — *paripācayet* ends both 1484b and 1484d, which looks like scribal repetition.
* 1483a is a syllable short for *anuṣṭubh*, 7 against 8. *Vārtākāntapradeśasya* would fix it, and the chapter does use *pradeśa* at 1472, but that is a guess; the line is worth checking against Shrigondekar's edition.
