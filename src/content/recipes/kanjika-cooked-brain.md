Brain is first poached in a sour fermented liquid and then fried with spices. Commercially cleaned calf or lamb brain avoids the source’s whole-head preparation.

## Ingredients

* 500 g cleaned calf or lamb brains  
* 500 ml lightly sour rice kāñjika, or 450 ml water plus 50 ml rice vinegar  
* 1 tsp salt  
* 30 g ghee or oil  
* ½ tsp ground cumin  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* ¼ tsp turmeric

## Method

1. Soak the brains in cold salted water for 30 minutes and remove any visible membrane.  
2. Poach gently in the kāñjika for 8–10 minutes, then lift out and cool.  
3. Cut into large pieces. Heat the ghee in a wide pan.  
4. Add the brain and fry carefully until lightly browned on the surfaces. Off the heat, turn the pieces through the cumin, pepper, and turmeric.  
5. Bloom the asafoetida in a teaspoon of hot ghee and pour it over.

**Notes**

* **Brain is a regulated tissue and the age of the animal is what matters.** In the UK and EU the skull and brain of sheep and goats over twelve months old, and of cattle over twelve months, are specified risk material and cannot legally enter the food chain; in the US the threshold for cattle is thirty months. Below those ages the same tissue is permitted. So calf and lamb brain from a butcher who can tell you the age of the animal are the versions to use — and the adult sheep's head that 1520 actually calls for is not available in either jurisdiction. Brain is also around 2 g of cholesterol per 100 g, which is worth knowing before serving 500 g of it.  
* Brain is delicate; keep the poach below a hard boil and turn the pieces gently. The 30-minute soak in cold salted water and the membrane removal are not in the text, but the *kāñjika* poach is: mild acid firms the protein, which is the same reason French practice poaches brains in a vinegar court-bouillon.  
* **The spices go on after the frying, not into it.** 1521 runs *punaḥ pacet* — cook it again — and only then *cūrṇaiḥ saṃyojya tat paścāt*, "having combined it with the powders, afterwards", and then *hiṅgudhūpena dhūpayet*. The earlier method had the spices heating in the ghee before the brain went in and the asafoetida among them as a dry spice, which inverts both the order and the technique: *dhūpayet* is fumigation, a final aromatic, and here it is the last word of the recipe.  
* *Cūrṇaiḥ* is bare — "with the powders" — so the specific spices are a reconstruction, presumably read across from 1503's pepper, cumin, and rock salt. The turmeric has no warrant in the passage at all.  
* *Yasya kasyāpi meṣādeḥ*, "of any animal whatever, beginning with the sheep", is a general licence rather than a choice between two animals: 1522–23 goes on to say the same for birds, and 1512 has already said that other wild animals are to be treated as sheep.  
* All 4 half-lines of 1520–1521 scan clean at 16 syllables.
