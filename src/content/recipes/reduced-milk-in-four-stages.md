The four stages are not just a ratio table — the text names each one by the class of food it lands in. At a half it is for drinking; at a third it is a *lehya*, thick enough to lick, the same consistency 1375 demands of *pāyasa*; at a sixth it sets into a lump; at an eighth it goes granular and the text calls it sugar. The last figure is not arbitrary. Milk is roughly one-eighth solids by weight, so an eighth of the starting volume is very nearly the point at which there is no water left to drive off.

## Ingredients

* 2 L whole milk, preferably buffalo milk  
* Optional: 50–100 g sugar, depending on intended use  
* Optional: ½ tsp cardamom

## Method

1. Pour the milk into a wide heavy saucepan and bring to a gentle simmer.  
2. Cook over the lowest practical heat, stirring and scraping the base and sides frequently.  
3. For drinkable reduced milk, stop at 1 L. For the lickable stage, stop at about 650 ml. For a mass that holds its shape, continue to about 330 ml. The eighth stage is around 250 ml, by which point the milk is essentially dry — expect it to turn grainy rather than fudgy, and to scorch unless the heat is very low.  
4. Add sugar or cardamom only near the end if desired.

**Notes**

* ***Lehyaka* is the chapter's own class term, not a loose description.** 1344 divides food into *bhojya*, *bhakṣya*, *peya*, *lehya*, and *coṣya* — chewed, morsels, drunk, licked, sucked — and 1375 gives the yardstick for the fourth, *pāyasa* "fit for licking". So the one-third stage has a specific target: thick enough to lick off a finger, falling off a spoon in a sheet.  
* **One-eighth is a floor, not an arbitrary stopping point.** Whole milk is about 12.5% solids by weight, so reducing to an eighth of the starting volume takes you to roughly the dry-matter limit. That is also why the last stage is called *śarkarā*: with the water gone, milk solids come out coarse and sandy, which is exactly the *danedar* grade of modern khoa. Fudge is the wrong texture for this stage.  
* The one-sixth stage is khoa or *māwā* — 330 ml from 2 L works out near 75% solids, squarely in the commercial range. The chapter probably has a name for it: at 1596 the king eats "curd, and then *ghana*", dense stuff, in a run of dairy dishes drawn from exactly these verses.  
* Weighing the pot before and during cooking is the easiest way to track reduction accurately.  
* **The half stage feeds the next recipe.** 1568 opens *ardhāvaśeṣite dugdhe*, picking up 1566's *ardhāvaśeṣam* word for word — the curd of 1568–70 is set from this milk, not from fresh milk, and everything downstream through *takra*, *majjikā*, and *śikhariṇī* inherits the extra concentration.  
* Sugar and cardamom are nowhere in 1565–67; they first appear at 1569, in the curd. *Śarkarā* at 1567 is the name of the fourth stage, not an ingredient added to it.
