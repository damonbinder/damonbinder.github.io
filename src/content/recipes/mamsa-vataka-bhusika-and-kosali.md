The passage runs two meat preparations side by side: one wet-ground on the stone until it is sticky like dough, the other only reduced to a crumb. The paste becomes a casing around a crumb centre, cooked in a pan (*māṃsa-vaṭaka*) or in oil (*bhūṣikā*); the crumb on its own, wrapped in wheat dough and roasted in embers, is *kośalī*.

## Ingredients

* 400 g finely minced lamb, goat, or pork — for the paste  
* 200 g lamb, goat, or pork, chopped a little coarser — for the *cūrṇa* centre  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida dissolved in 30 ml water  
* 1½ tsp salt  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ½ tsp ground coriander  
* 20 g ghee, for *māṃsa-vaṭakas*  
* 500 ml oil, for *bhūṣikās*  
* 250 g plain wheat dough, for *kośalīs*

## Method

1. Work the 400 g of fine mince with the asafoetida-water, two thirds of the salt, and the cumin, pepper, and coriander — by hand, in a mortar, or in a processor — until it is smooth, tacky, and holds together like a stiff dough. This is the casing.  
2. Season the coarser 200 g with the remaining salt and keep it loose and crumbly. This is the *cūrṇa* centre.  
3. Divide the paste into 12. Flatten each piece in a wet palm, put about 15 g of the crumb in the middle, close the paste over it, seal the seam, and roll it smooth.  
4. For *māṃsa-vaṭakas*, brown the balls in the ghee over medium heat, add 100 ml water, cover, and cook for 12–15 minutes, turning once, until the centre reads 71°C.  
5. For *bhūṣikās*, deep-fry the same balls at 165°C for 8–10 minutes, until deep brown and 71°C at the centre. Or cook them as in step 4 and then fry them briefly for color.  
6. For *kośalīs*, use the crumb alone and no casing. Roll the dough thin, enclose 40–50 g portions, seal the edges well, and roast in embers or bake at 220°C for 15–20 minutes, until the dough is browned and the filling reads 71°C.

**Notes**

* **The filling is named in the text, and the wrapping goes the other way round from how it looks.** 1480a–b reads *cūrṇīkṛtaṃ tu yanmāṃsaṃ golakais tad viveṣṭayet* — "the meat which has been reduced to *cūrṇa*, that (*tat*, accusative) one should envelop with the balls (*golakaiḥ*, instrumental)". The accusative is what gets enveloped and the instrumental is the wrapper, so the paste balls of 1479 are the casing and a second, drier meat preparation is the core, not the reverse. The chapter uses *vi-veṣṭ-* the same way elsewhere: at 1492 the intestines (accusative) are wound onto a spit-rod, and at 1482 the *cūrṇita* meat is *kaṇikā-pariveṣṭita*, wrapped around with dough. 1480c then calls the results *cūrṇagarbhān vaṭakān*, "*cūrṇa*-cored *vaṭakas*".
* **The two meat preparations differ in texture, not in seasoning.** The paste is *piṣṭavac cikaṇaṃ*, sticky like dough; the core is *cūrṇīkṛta*, reduced to particles. The spicing cannot be what separates them, because 1479 has already put the powders into the paste. The 400/200 split and the light salting of the crumb are mine; the text gives no proportions and says nothing about seasoning the core.
* **1485 confirms which of the two does the stuffing.** The stuffed vegetables of the next recipe are filled with *tena māṃsena*, "that meat", which 1485c then names *cūrṇamāṃsena* — the crumb, not the paste. So the crumb is this chapter's standing stuffing material.
* *Kaṇikā* (1482) is wheat flour or wheat dough: the Marathi gloss is "wheaten flour, whether fine flour or meal", and the Kannada lexica give "dough made of wheat flour, a premix for making different food items". A dough parcel also suits the name, since *kośa* is a sheath or pod.
* *Bhūṣikā* is transparently from *bhūṣā*, ornament, and 1481 calls the *vaṭakas* *dṛśyā manoharāḥ*, handsome to look at and delightful — so the fried version may well be named for how it looks. 1481 also says *ta eva vaṭakāḥ*, "those very same *vaṭakas*", so the *bhūṣikās* are the same objects at the same size, not smaller ones. That is why the frying oil here runs cooler and longer than usual: a 50 g filled ball at 175°C is black outside before the core is safe.
* *Āṇaka*, the vessel of 1480, is not securely identified. The lexica give *āṇaka*/*ānaka* as a large drum rather than a pot, and the chapter always sets it against two other options — deep oil, and the bare fire (1487, 1521, 1532, 1539, where the fire is twice specified as smokeless). So it is a closed or drum-shaped vessel used with little fat. "Pan" is safe; a covered pan is probably closer, which is what step 4 uses.
* **The three dishes are alternatives, not a sequence.** A cook makes one. The ghee, the frying oil, and the dough are each for a single variant, and *kośalī* needs no paste at all.
* Check the centre of every ball. A filled ball is thicker than a plain one, and the crumb core is the last part to come up to temperature.
* **The passage shares verse 1478 with the preceding recipe, and the seam runs down the middle of it.** 1478a–b closes the *Roast-Meat Salad* — *tanmāṃsaṃ*, "that meat", points back to the ember-roasted meat of 1476a. 1478c–d, *āmaṃ māṃsaṃ ca peṣaṇyāṃ hiṅgutoyena siñcitam*, opens this recipe and has no finite verb of its own until *peṣayed* in 1479b. Both recipes print the whole verse and translate only their own half.
