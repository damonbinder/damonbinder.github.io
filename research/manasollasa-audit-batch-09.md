# Mānasollāsa 3.13.1559–1574 — the pickles and the dairy chain

*Created 2026-07-25 12:07.*

Scope: seven recipes — `mustard-oil-fruit-pickles.md` (1559–60), `pickled-shoots-and-roots.md` (1561–64), `reduced-milk-in-four-stages.md` (1565–67), `dadhi-curd-from-reduced-milk.md` (1568–70), `spiced-takra.md` (1571–72, **published**), `majjika-sweet-spiced-buttermilk.md` (1573, **published**), `sikharini-sweet-strained-curd.md` (1574). ## Summary

**Six of the seven had substantive errors. `spiced-takra.md` is the exception, and it is clean in the place that matters most: its dilution ratios are right.** I can now confirm them against an external control rather than internal reasoning — 1571–72's `निर्जलं मथितं` / `जलार्धकम्` / `पादाम्बु` reproduces the standard Ayurvedic four- or five-fold classification of churned dairy term for term (*ghola* fat-in and waterless, *mathita* fat-out and waterless, *takra* a quarter water, *udaśvit* half water). Damon's published note carries it correctly, and every downstream ratio that depends on it stands.

**The most consequential correction is in the other published file.** `majjika-sweet-spiced-buttermilk.md` offered "500 ml fresh buttermilk **or takra**" — but 1573 specifies `मथितं`, and *mathita* and *takra* are the two ends of the very scale the previous verse has just defined. The recipe was offering the quarter-water product as an equivalent for the no-water one. 1596 corroborates from the other direction: in the order of a royal meal the king *sucks* the *majjikā* (`चूषेन्मज्जिकां`), he does not drink it, which puts it in the `coṣya` class of 1344 and makes it a thick spoonable sweet rather than a thin drink. Damon's own practice — Greek yoghurt thinned to the consistency he wanted — is closer to the text than his ingredient line was.

**The chain break the brief predicted is real, and it has the same fingerprint as the one in the Śuṇṭhaka audit — the number 750.** `sikharini-sweet-strained-curd.md` asked for 750 g of curd. The parent, 1568–70, sets 500 ml. And *takra* and *majjikā* both want the whole batch too, because the three are alternative treatments of one setting of curd, not a sequence. Dropped to 500 g, with sugar rescaled 80 → 55 g.

**Two things to know before cooking.** First, `कर्पूरधूपितं` in the *majjikā* is camphor **smoke** — fumigation of the vessel, not a speck stirred in, exactly as Damon already reads `dhūpita` for the cumin and asafoetida in the *takra*. That delivers a trace rather than a measurable dose, which may make the recipe cookable after all given he hasn't found edible camphor he trusts. Second, the fruit pickle: green mango is acidic enough to look after itself, but bitter gourd, jackfruit, and green banana sit near pH 6, and produce held under a layer of oil is anaerobic. That is the garlic-in-oil risk profile. I raised the salt from 4% to 8% and added an explicit note that this is a refrigerated few-weeks pickle, not a preserve.

**One good result on the dairy chemistry, which cuts against an existing note.** `dadhi-curd-from-reduced-milk.md` hedged that six *yāmas* (≈18 hours) "may reflect a different time reckoning". No hedge is needed. Milk reduced to half carries double the casein, double the lactose, and **double the buffering capacity**, so substantially more acid has to be made before the pH reaches 4.6 and the gel sets. Eighteen hours at an ambient 25–30 °C with a spoonful of *takra* as inoculum is a plausible set, and the text supplies the shorter option itself in a clause the translation had dropped (`हीनकाले तथा पथ्यं`, "in a shorter time it is likewise wholesome").

## 1. The recipes that carry a technical classification

Worth taking first, because three of my seven turn on it and the brief flagged it as defect 5.

1344 gives the fivefold scheme — `भोज्यं भक्ष्यं तथा पेयं लेह्यं चोष्यं` — and the source file's own English already unpacks it. 1375 gives the yardstick for the fourth class: `पायसं लेहने योग्यं`, *pāyasa* "fit for licking". What I had not expected is that **the chapter's closing serving order at 1596–97 walks the whole dairy chain through that taxonomy, verb by verb**:

`पिबेच्च पानकं हृद्यं लिह्याच्छिखरिणीमपि । चूषेन्मज्जिकां पश्चाद्दधि चाद्यात्ततो घनम् ॥ ततस्तक्रमश्नीयात्सैन्धवेन च संयुतम् । क्षीरं वापि पिबेत्…`

- `पिबेत् पानकं` — *drink* the *pānaka* → `peya`
- `लिह्यात् शिखरिणीम्` — **lick** the *śikhariṇī* → `lehya`
- `चूषेत् मज्जिकाम्` — **suck** the *majjikā* → `coṣya`
- `अद्यात् दधि` — *eat* the curd, `ततो घनम्` — then the dense stuff
- `अश्नीयात् तक्रम्` — *eat* the *takra*, with rock salt
- `पिबेत् क्षीरम्` — *drink* the milk → `peya`

So the chapter tells you the consistency of four of my five dairy products, in its own vocabulary, sixty verses later. Two consequences I applied:

- **1567's `लेह्यकं` is a class term, not a texture adjective**, and it had been flattened to "spoonable". The one-third reduction stage is a `lehya`, targeted at the same consistency 1375 asks of *pāyasa*.
- **`घन` at 1596d is probably the chapter's name for the one-sixth stage** — `पिण्डताम् एति`, "attains the state of a lump". Medium confidence, noted rather than asserted.

## 2. Per-recipe findings

### 1559–1560 — Fruit pickles in mustard and oil

**CONFIRMED**

1. **`अथवा` dropped.** The verse opens "or else", and it is the hinge of the whole pickling section: 1554–58 has just put a long list of fruits, pods, and gourds into `लवणाम्भसि`, salt water, and 1559 offers the same produce a mustard-and-oil pack instead. 1561–64 then runs both options again for shoots and roots. Restored, and noted.
2. **The reconstruction contradicted `वृन्तसहितम्`.** The text says place the fruit *together with its stalk*; the method said "cut into bite-sized pieces". This is not decorative — an intact stem plug is what stops a whole green mango splitting and spoiling at the shoulder, and it is how whole-mango pickles are still made. Note that the qualifier belongs only to 1559's fruits; the bitter gourd, jackfruit, and banana of 1560 carry none, so cutting those is fine. Method now splits the two.
3. **Turmeric and fenugreek are inventions.** Neither is in 1559–60. Turmeric appears elsewhere in the chapter (`हरिद्रा` 1444, `निशा` 1449) and *sour* turmeric is in 1563 — but as a thing being pickled, not a spice. Both kept as optional and labelled modern.
4. **Salt was too low to do the job the text gives it.** 40 g on 1 kg is 4%. The text names salt as the only preservative, and three of the four named vegetables are low-acid. Raised to 80 g (8%), which is the low end of traditional Indian oil-pickle practice, with a note that ambient-stable oil pickles get there with 10–15% salt plus hard sun-drying and that this one is a refrigerated few-weeks pickle. Also specified that the oil should stand a centimetre clear above the pressed-down solids; 250 ml on 1 kg only just manages it.

**JUDGEMENT CALLS** *(both applied, both flagged, both easy to revert)*

- **J1 — the oil is not mustard oil, and the title said it was.** Two arguments. (a) Unqualified `तैल` in Sanskrit means sesame oil: the word is derived from `तिल`, and the Ayurvedic convention, stated outright in the Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā, is that an unspecified oil is sesame. (b) Stronger, and internal: the companion verse 1562 asks for `सराजिके तैले`, "oil **with mustard in it**", which is only informative if the oil is not itself pressed from mustard. The mustard here is `राजिकाचूर्ण`, powder — a separate ingredient from the medium. **I changed the title from "Mustard-Oil Fruit Pickles" to "Fruit Pickles in Mustard and Oil"** and made the ingredient "250 ml sesame oil, or mustard oil — see notes". Mustard oil is the living South Asian pickle medium and the recipe works with it; what was wrong was the title asserting it as the source's.
- **J2 — mustard raised 40 → 80 g.** The fruit is placed *in* `राजिकाचूर्णे`, a locative of the medium: mustard powder is the pack, not a seasoning. 40 g per kg is a dusting. 80 g is in line with modern *rai*-based pickle masalas and still conservative against *avakaya*-scale recipes.

Nothing else in the passage is missing. `कारवेल्ल` (bitter gourd, *Momordica charantia*), `पनस` (jackfruit), `कदलीफल` (banana), and `चूत` (mango, also at 1557) are all secure and all present.

### 1561–1564 — Pickled shoots and roots

**CONFIRMED**

1. **`मृदून्` was read as an instruction when it is an adjective — and that manufactured a cooking step the text does not have.** `प्ररोहान् क्षालितान् मृदून्` is three accusative plurals in agreement: "the sprouts, washed, tender". The translation read it as "Wash and **soften**", and the method then boiled everything. The text asks you to pick shoots that are *already* tender and wash them; `क्षालितान्` is the only preparation in the passage, matching `प्रक्षाल्य` at 1559. The clinching point is that when this author means "having made them X" he writes `कृत्वा`, which is exactly what he does two verses later at 1564 (`एतानि पूर्ववत्कृत्वा`). **I corrected the translation and kept the boiling, relabelled as a modern safety addition** — bamboo shoots carry cyanogenic glycosides and elephant-foot yam calcium-oxalate raphides, so it has to stay.
2. **Five items dropped from the translation.** 1563 lists seven things and the translation carried two. Missing: `मागिणीमाकं`, `पैष्टुं`, `वनमागिणीम्`, `कर्पूरमागिणीमूलं`. And from 1564, `मधुशिग्रुम्` — **the securely identifiable one**, "sweet *śigru*", the sweet or red variety of the drumstick tree, given in the lexica as *Moringa concanensis*. All restored, unidentified ones transliterated and asterisked per policy.
3. **"Firm radish" was invented.** `मूलक` is at 1454 and 1548, not in 1561–64. Removed from the ingredient list; drumstick pods added in its place, since those are actually in the text. Turmeric also removed (not in the passage).

**JUDGEMENT CALL**

- **J3 — `वा` in 1564d.** `तैले वापि विनिक्षिपेत्` keeps the brine of 1562 available for the roots as well; the translation had "and placed in oil", turning an alternative into the sole method. Rendered "into oil as well". Also made `पूर्ववत्` explicit in the notes rather than paraphrased as "in the same way".

**UNRESOLVED — six terms, all left transliterated and asterisked**

| Term | Best lead | Confidence |

**Secure and now stated in the notes:** `सूरण` = elephant-foot yam (*Amorphophallus paeoniifolius*); `कचोर` = zedoary (*Curcuma zedoaria*), and `kacora` is specifically the **Kannada** form of the name, which independently supports reading `मागिणी` as vernacular; `अम्लहरिद्रका` "sour turmeric" = mango-ginger (*Curcuma amada*), still a standard South Indian pickling rhizome; `शतावरी` = *Asparagus racemosus*, young shoots eaten.

**Safety note added rather than a foraging suggestion:** the traditional pungent part of the drumstick tree, and probably what 1564 means, is the **root** — but moringa root bark contains spirochin and is not regarded as safe in quantity, so the pods stand in. Damon's existing "should not be gathered from the wild on the basis of the Sanskrit names alone" is kept and strengthened to bold.

### 1565–1567 — Reduced milk in four stages

The reconstruction's arithmetic is right: from 2 L, the four stages land at 1000 / 667 / 333 / 250 ml, and 650 / 330 / 250 are all correct. The four the text names *are* enumerable and *do* match. What was wrong is what the four are called.

**CONFIRMED**

1. **`लेह्यकं` flattened to "spoonable".** See §1. This is the chapter's own class term and it has a stated yardstick at 1375. Rendered "a *lehya*, a thing to be licked" and cross-referenced in the notes.
2. **`शर्करा स्यात्` rendered as "a fudge-like confection".** Wrong texture. `शर्करा` means granulated sugar and, at root, grit — and that is exactly what happens: with the water gone, milk solids crystallize out coarse and sandy. This is the *danedar* grade of modern khoa. Fudge is smooth; the text is telling you it goes grainy.
3. Minor restorations: `स्थाल्याम्` (in a pot) and `दर्वी` (with a ladle), both dropped. The phrase `पचेत्स्थाल्यां मृदावग्नौ दर्वीघट्टन-` is the same anti-scorching protocol the prior audit identified at 1446.

**JUDGEMENT CALL**

- **J4 — the intro now makes a numerical claim I should flag.** Whole milk is about 12.5% solids by weight, i.e. roughly one-eighth. So `अष्टावशेषिक` — reduce to one-eighth of the starting volume — is very nearly the dry-matter limit of milk, which explains both why the series ends there and why the terminal product is granular. Sanity check on the other stages, taking 2 L ≈ 2060 g with ~262 g of solids: the half stage is ~25% solids (evaporated-milk/*basundi* territory, drinkable ✓); the third ~38% (thick, lickable ✓); the sixth ~75% (squarely commercial khoa ✓); the eighth ~87% (very hard *batti* khoa, right at the edge of what a pot can do). **All four of the text's descriptions land where the text says they do.** That is a good sign for the transcription. I put the one-eighth point in the intro; it is an inference, not something the text states.

Also noted: sugar and cardamom are nowhere in 1565–67 — they first appear at 1569, in the curd — and `शर्करा` at 1567 is the *name* of the fourth stage, not an ingredient added to it. The optional sugar in the ingredient list is fine but the translation now makes the distinction unmissable.

### 1568–1570 — *Dadhi* from reduced milk

**CONFIRMED**

1. **The starter is `तक्र`, and the word was dropped.** `तक्रमीषद्विनिक्षिपेत्` — "one should throw in a little *takra*" — rendered as "a little buttermilk". Right in substance, but it hides the most interesting structural fact in my whole set: *takra* is not defined until 1572, three verses later, and it is made **by churning this very curd**. The chain loops. The chapter uses *takra* as a dairy seed elsewhere too — at 1409 sour *takra* is the coagulant that curdles milk for *kṣīraprakāra*, which Damon's own draft of that recipe already notes matters for texture.
2. **`हीनकाले तथा पथ्यं` dropped.** 1570d is a two-part statement: shorter time, likewise wholesome; long time, much sourness. Only the second half survived. This bears directly on the recipe's own hedge about the six *yāmas* — the text already gives you the shorter set.
3. **Three praise epithets dropped.** `पथ्यं मृष्टं मनोहरम्` — wholesome, delicious, charming — where `पथ्य` is a technical Ayurvedic term, and its recurrence in the next pāda is what makes the timing clause a health claim rather than a taste note. Restored.

**JUDGEMENT CALL**

- **J5 — incubation 6–10 h → 8–12 h**, with a line telling the cook to expect it to take longer than a plain-milk set. See the buffering argument in the summary. The existing note speculating about "a different time reckoning or ambient temperature" is replaced with the chemistry.

Also restored to the notes: the text specifies a **new** pot (`नवस्थाल्यां`) and a draught-free spot, and says nothing about cooling the milk or naming a temperature — both of those are modern.

**One forward-looking consequence I flagged in the file.** Simmering milk down to half far exceeds the 85 °C hold used on modern yoghurt milk, so the whey proteins are fully denatured and complexed with the casein. The gel is dense and resists syneresis. So the text's *dadhi* not only sets — it sets **firmer than plain-milk yoghurt, and sheds less whey**. That matters two recipes downstream, and it matters for *mastu*: see §4.

### 1571–1572 — Spiced *takra* (published)

**No substantive errors.** The ratios are right, the classification is right, and Damon's note 3 correctly observes that neither *mathita* nor *udaśvit* is spiced — which the Sanskrit confirms, since `धूपितं`, `समायुक्तम्`, and `चूर्णितम्` are all neuter singular in agreement with `तक्रम्` alone.

The external control: the medical texts' classification of churned dairy is *ghola* (fat left in, no water), *mathita* (fat removed, no water), *takra* (a quarter water), *udaśvit* (half water) — matching `निर्जलं मथितं` / `पादाम्बु` / `जलार्धकम्` slot for slot. So 1571–72 is reproducing a standing Ayurvedic scheme, not improvising, and the "add a quarter / a half of its volume" reading (rather than "a quarter / half of the total") is the mainstream one.

Two minor omissions restored, and two notes appended. Verbatim log in §5. The ingredient list is internally consistent with Damon's substitution: cultured buttermilk standing in for *mathita*, plus 125 ml water on 500 ml = exactly the quarter the text asks for. Left alone.

### 1573 — *Majjikā* (published)

**CONFIRMED**

1. **`मथितं` → "Churned curd or buttermilk", and the ingredient list offered *takra*.** The head word of the verse is the defined technical term for the **undiluted** member of the triad the previous verse has just laid out. Offering *takra* as an alternative offers the opposite end of the scale. Translation now leads with `*Mathita* [the undiluted churned curd of 1571]`; ingredient line now reads "500 ml fresh buttermilk, or *mathita* (undiluted churned curd)", keeping Damon's lead ingredient and his substitution untouched.
2. **`कर्पूरधूपितं` is smoke, and the method stirs it in.** The recipe says "Add the smallest possible speck of edible camphor". That contradicts how Damon's own *takra* file reads `dhūpita` two verses earlier ("aromatics carried on hot fat rather than stirred in raw"). Fumigating the vessel with burning camphor is the text's technique, and it deposits a trace rather than a dose. **I did not change the method** — that is his sourcing decision and his note about not trusting available camphor stands — but I added it as a note, because it may make the recipe cookable for him.

**JUDGEMENT CALL**

- **J6 — I added a `**Notes**` section**, which the file did not have. Two bullets. Justified because the `mathita`/`takra` point needs somewhere to live and because the camphor-as-smoke point is actionable; but it is an insertion into a published file and easy to remove.

The verse gives no praise epithets and nothing else is missing. `नाम्ना … इत्यभिधीयते` is carried.

### 1574 — *Śikhariṇī*

**CONFIRMED**

1. **750 g → 500 g.** The chain break; see §4. Sugar rescaled 80 → 55 g to hold the ratio.
2. **`दधि` is a back-reference.** `निष्पीड्य दधि वस्त्रेण` presses *the dadhi* — the specific product of 1570, set from half-reduced milk — not generic curd. ## 3. Stress-testing the cooking

**Will curd set from reduced milk?** Yes, and better than from plain milk. Doubling the solids doubles the casein, so the gel is denser; the extended simmer fully denatures β-lactoglobulin, which then complexes with κ-casein and gives a firm gel that resists weeping. This is why *mishti doi* is made from reduced milk. The cost is time, not failure: double the buffering capacity means more acid is needed to reach pH 4.6. Nothing in this chain is at risk of not setting.

**Will anything split or grain?** No. Unlike the sour curd sauce of 1442–48 that the prior audit worried about, nothing in 1565–74 combines heat with acid. The reduction happens before culturing; everything after culturing is cold. The one texture warning worth carrying is at the *other* end — the one-eighth reduction stage is *supposed* to go grainy, and the recipe was describing it as fudge.

**The pickles.** Written up under 1559–60 above. In short: the salt was doing less work than the text assigns it, three of the four named vegetables are low-acid, oil cover is anaerobic, and refrigeration was silently carrying the whole load. Raised to 8% salt, oil cover specified, and the limits stated plainly in a note. The shoots-and-roots brine at 60 g/L (6%) with 2–3 days ambient then refrigeration is sound as written, and I left it.

## 4. Does the dairy chain connect?

**Yes, end to end, after one fix.** Here is the chain with the linking words that make it a chain, which is the part the translations had been losing.

**Three of the five links are explicit and were partly or wholly invisible in the translations:** 1568's verbatim repetition of `अर्धावशेषं`, 1571's bare `तत्`, and 1573's `मथितं`. The fourth, 1574's `दधि`, was rendered as generic "curd". All four now say what they refer to.

**The ratios connect.** 500 ml of *dadhi* at ~7% fat (whole milk reduced 2×) churns to roughly 40 g of butter and ~485 ml of *mathita*; a quarter of that is ~121 ml, giving ~605 ml of *takra*. The published `spiced-takra.md` asks for 500 ml + 125 ml water = 625 ml, which matches both the ratio and the yield. `majjika` asks for 500 ml, which is one batch of *mathita*. Both fine.

**The one break was `śikhariṇī` at 750 g against a 500 ml parent — fixed.** The structural point is the same one the prior audit made about the four *Cakkalikās*: *takra*, *majjikā*, and *śikhariṇī* are **parallel** treatments of one setting of curd, not a sequence, so each should take the whole batch and the batch size should be one number across the whole family. That number is 500 ml, set twice over in the two published files. `śikhariṇī` was the only sibling asking for more than the parent produces, and it was asking for half again as much — a precise repeat of the Sour Meat Glaze's 750 g against a ~550 g parent.

### Implication for `mastu-spiced-whey.md` (1575 — agent 10's file, not edited)

**`mastu` asks for 500 ml of whey, and the chain cannot supply it — before or after my change.**

- Draining 750 g of ordinary yoghurt yields ~375–430 ml of whey. Already short of 500 ml.
- Draining 500 g (the corrected figure) yields ~250–285 ml.
- Draining the text's actual material — 525 g of reduced-milk *dadhi* — yields **less still**, ~235 ml, because the fully denatured whey proteins are bound into the gel and the curd holds its water. This is the one place where the reduced-milk chemistry works against you.

**Recommendation for agent 10: drop `mastu` to ~250 ml of whey**, which is one *śikhariṇī* batch, and note that a reduced-milk *dadhi* drains less freely than yoghurt. The tempering (10 g ghee, ½ tsp cumin, ¾ tsp salt) should come down proportionately. Also: 1575b `जीरकाज्यसैन्धवैः` scans at **7 syllables**, one short — the only metrical defect anywhere in 1565–75 — so that half-verse is worth collating.

Also worth passing on: `स्रावितं यद्धृतं तोयं` is `yad **dhṛtaṃ**`, "the water which, drained off, **is retained**" — the participle is doing real work, marking the whey as kept rather than discarded, which is the whole point of the recipe existing. The existing translation's "The liquid retained after curd has drained" gets there.
