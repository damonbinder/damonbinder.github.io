## Ingredients

* 400 g boneless lamb, pork, or goat, cut into 2 cm pieces  
* 150 g eggplant, cut into 2 cm slices  
* 150 g daikon radish, cut into 2 cm pieces  
* 25 g fresh ginger, cut into 2 cm pieces  
* 30 ml oil  
* ½ tsp turmeric  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* 1 tsp ground coriander  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ½ tsp ground dried ginger  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* ½ tsp ground fenugreek  
* 1½ tsp salt  
* 15 g tamarind concentrate, 40 ml pomegranate juice, and 60 ml water, mixed together  
* Small handful of bitter greens — fenugreek or mustard leaves — shredded  
* Optional: 150 g of a second meat or offal, cut to the same size

For the *vaṭakas*

* 150 g sprouted mung beans  
* 1 small onion, roughly chopped  
* 15 g fresh ginger, chopped  
* ½ tsp salt  
* Oil for shallow-frying

## Method

1. For the *vaṭakas*, blitz the sprouted mung with the onion, ginger, and salt to a thick, coarse paste, adding no water. Drop heaped teaspoons into 1 cm of hot oil and fry until browned on both sides, then drain. Ready-made moong dal vada can stand in.  
2. Toss the meat with the turmeric, cumin, coriander, pepper, dried ginger, and asafoetida until evenly coated.  
3. Heat the 30 ml of oil in a wide pan until it shimmers. Fry the meat with the 2 cm ginger pieces over high heat until browned all over.  
4. Add the radish, eggplant, fenugreek powder, shredded greens, and salt all together. Keep the heat high and toss frequently — the pan stays uncovered throughout — until the vegetables are tender and everything is well cooked, *susiddham*.  
5. Stir in the *vaṭakas* along with the tamarind, pomegranate juice, and water mixture, and toss until warmed through and coated. The dish should stay moist without pooling liquid.

**Notes**

* Coffee senna (*Senna occidentalis*, also called *kasondi*) is a bitter pot-herb eaten in South India. It isn't sold outside India, so I've suggested substituting either mustard or fenugreek leaves instead.
