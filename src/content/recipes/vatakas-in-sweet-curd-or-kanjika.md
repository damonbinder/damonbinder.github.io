Two alternatives, not a combination: the same fried cakes go either into sweetened churned curd or into sour *kāñjika*, and are named after whichever they land in. The cakes are soaked after frying so that they soften.

## Ingredients

* 8 fried holeless *ghārikās*, made from iḍerikā batter (see *Ghārikā Black-Gram Rings*, shaped without the holes)  
* For the sweet curd: 500 g plain yoghurt, 40 g sugar, ½ tsp ground cardamom, ¼ tsp ground black pepper  
* For the kāñjika: 500 ml lightly sour rice water or thin fermented rice drink, ½ tsp salt

## Method

1. Fry holeless cakes from the fermented urad batter as for the *ghārikās*, and drain. Without the holes the centre cooks more slowly, so make them a little thinner.  
2. For the sweet version, whisk the yoghurt hard with the sugar, cardamom, and pepper until it is smooth and pourable. Add no water.  
3. For the sour version, season the kāñjika lightly with salt.  
4. While the cakes are still warm, immerse them in the chosen liquid. Chill or rest for at least 1 hour before serving.

**Notes**

* **Add no water to the curd.** The text says *mathita*, and the chapter defines the word itself at 1571: churn curd, take off the butter, and the undiluted product is *mathita*; cut with half its volume of water it is *udaśvit*; with a quarter, *takra*. So *mathita* is specifically the waterless one, and thinning yoghurt by a quarter would make this the wrong preparation by the chapter's own definition. Whisking alone loosens yoghurt enough to pour. Since the butter has been removed, low-fat yoghurt is marginally closer than whole-milk.  
* The soaking liquid is almost exactly the chapter's own *majjikā* at 1573 — *mathita* with sugar and cardamom powder — with pepper here in place of the camphor there.  
* The naming rule is stated outright in the next verse (1405): *yatra yatra dravadravye tannāmnā vaṭakās tu te*, "in whatever liquid substance they are put, the *vaṭakas* take their name from it." That is why the same cake is a *vaṭikā* in curd and a *kāñjika* in *kāñjika*.  
* This is structurally similar to later dahi vada, but the source's dairy version is sweet rather than salty.  
* A simple kāñjika can be made by fermenting cooked rice and water at room temperature for 24–48 hours, but use clean equipment and refrigerate once pleasantly sour.  
* 1404 specifies no seasoning at all for the *kāñjika* version. The salt follows the chapter's own *dhūpa-kāñjika* at 1580, which is salted.
