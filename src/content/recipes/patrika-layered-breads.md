*Patrikās* are thin sheets cooked with fat on a griddle and then stacked one above another. The dough is not a new one: the `गोलान्` of 1385 are the *maṇḍaka* dough-balls of 1376–78, enriched with milk and a little ghee. A restrained amount of ghee keeps the result closer to the source than a modern flaky paratha.

## Ingredients

* 1 batch *maṇḍaka* dough (from 300 g fine wheat flour, made with milk and water)  
* 50 g ghee, melted  
* Rice flour, for dusting

## Method

1. Divide the dough into 10 balls, dust with rice flour, and roll each as thinly as possible.  
2. Brush a griddle lightly with ghee and cook each sheet for about 30 seconds per side, keeping it pale.  
3. Brush each cooked sheet with a trace of ghee and stack them immediately.  
4. Return the stack to the griddle for 30–60 seconds a side, pressing lightly, so the sheets set together.  
5. Cut the stack into wedges or serve whole.

**Notes**

* **The griddle is in the text.** Shrigondekar's printed edition reads `ताप्यां` at 1385d, the locative of *tāpī*, the hot plate — the same word used at 1390 and 1393, and the ancestor of the modern *tavā*. The transcription in the `original` field above has lost the ending and reads `ताप्य`, which looks like a gerund, "having heated". The griddle is what distinguishes *patrikās* from the deep-fried *sohalā* and *pāhalikā* of the preceding verses.  
* **The stack is probably cooked, not just assembled.** The finite verb `विपचेत्` comes *after* `उपर्युपरि निक्षिप्ताः`, and it governs `पत्रिकाः`, which is what the stacking participle agrees with; the earlier `पाचितान्` agrees with the dough-balls instead. On that reading the sheets are cooked once individually and the pile is then cooked again. [Judgement call: read the other way, `विपचेत्` is simply the main verb for the whole operation, the sheets are stacked for service, and step 4 can be dropped.]  
* *Patrikā* means "little leaf" — the name is about the thinness of the sheet.  
* The text says `स्नेहेन`, "with fat", not specifically ghee; sesame oil would be equally in period.  
* Using sifted flour reflects the *maṇḍaka* instruction to remove coarse particles. The coarse fraction is not waste: 1386–87 makes it into *kīsāra*.  
* Also collated: 1385b reads `मृद्वः` in the printed edition, not `मृदवः` as transcribed above, which is why that half-verse currently scans one syllable long.
