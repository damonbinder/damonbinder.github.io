A sweet, cardamom-scented buttermilk drink. Buttermilk is hard to find where I am, so I substituted Greek yoghurt thinned with water to the consistency I wanted and sweetened to taste. I have not yet found camphor that I trust to eat.

## Ingredients

* 500 ml fresh buttermilk, or *mathita* (undiluted churned curd)  
* 40 g sugar  
* ½ tsp ground cardamom  
* Optional: a speck of food-grade edible camphor

## Method

1. Whisk the buttermilk with the sugar and cardamom until the sugar dissolves.  
2. Add the smallest possible speck of edible camphor if using.  
3. Chill well and serve.

**Notes**

* The base is *mathita*, not *takra*. 1571 defines *mathita* as the churned curd with **no** water added and *takra* as the same thing cut with a quarter of its volume of water, so *majjikā* is the thick member of the family. 1596 agrees: in the order of a royal meal the king *sucks* the *majjikā* (*cūṣen majjikām*) rather than drinking it, which places it in the *coṣya* class of 1344.  
* *Karpūra-dhūpitam* is camphor **smoke**. As with the cumin and asafoetida in the *takra*, *dhūpita* points to an aromatic carried to the food rather than stirred into it — the vessel is perfumed with burning camphor and the drink takes up a trace. That delivers far less camphor than a speck stirred in, which is worth knowing given how hard trustworthy edible camphor is to find.
