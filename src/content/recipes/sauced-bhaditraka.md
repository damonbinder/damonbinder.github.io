## Ingredients

* 750 g lamb or pork, cut into bite-sized pieces  
* 100 ml tamarind water or diluted pomegranate juice  
* 30 ml ginger juice  
* 30 ml coriander-leaf juice or 3 Tb finely chopped coriander  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* ½ tsp fenugreek powder  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* 1½ tsp salt  
* 30 g ghee  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper

## Method

1. Pierce the meat pieces repeatedly with a skewer or fork, as for the skewered *bhaditraka*.  
2. Combine the meat with the sour liquid, ginger, coriander, cumin, fenugreek, asafoetida, and salt in a wide pan.  
3. Cook uncovered over low heat until the liquid has evaporated and the meat is cooked through.  
4. Add the ghee and fry the pieces until lightly browned. Finish with black pepper.

**Notes**

* **This is the same preparation as the skewered *bhaditraka*, taken a different way.** 1466 opens *anenaiva prakāreṇa*, "in this very same way", and *jarjarīkṛtya khaṇḍakān* — "having riddled the pieces with holes" — repeats the piercing of 1463. Only the cooking differs: a pot instead of embers. The text keeps the name too, calling the finished dish *bhaḍitraka* fried in a pan (*bhṛṣṭe bhāṇḍe bhaḍitrake*, 1468).
* The back-reference implies the loin of 1462, but a braise-and-fry suits a harder-working cut, so the cut here is left open.
* No salt is named in 1466–1468. Either it carries over from the rock salt worked into the pieces at 1463, or it is simply assumed; the 1½ tsp here is the reconstruction's.
