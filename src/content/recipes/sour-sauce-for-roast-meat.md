translation: |-
  He should mix tamarind into curd, or else pomegranate essence; or he should combine ground emblic myrobalan, or *amlavetasa*; or the juice of citron, and the juice of *dantaśaṭha*. Or he may mix these juices, two or three of them, as he pleases.

  For aroma he should put in there coriander, asafoetida, and cumin; and turmeric for colour; and likewise very finely cut fresh ginger. For relish he should put in dried ginger, black pepper, and rock salt. He should strain it through a white cloth, and mix in a little oil.

  Having put it into an earthenware pot, stirring it first with a ladle, the one skilled in cooking should cook it over a gentle fire into a *pralehaka*.

  And having thrown the *śuṇṭhaka*s in there, he should make them tender by the cooking. When they are steeped in all the juices and done, the wise one should take them off the fire.

  The expert should perfume it with asafoetida, or again with *nakhadhūpa*, or with the smoke of cumin, or with *śaśidhūpa*.

## Ingredients

* 500 g cooked roast pork, lamb, or goat  
* 300 g plain yoghurt  
* 30 g tamarind concentrate  
* 40 ml pomegranate molasses, or 100 ml pomegranate juice reduced to 40 ml  
* 30 ml lemon juice  
* 1 tsp ground coriander  
* 1 tsp ground cumin  
* ½ tsp turmeric  
* 15 g fresh ginger, very finely cut  
* ½ tsp ground dried ginger  
* ½ tsp ground black pepper  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida  
* 1½ tsp rock salt  
* 20 ml Indian sesame oil, or another neutral oil  
* To finish: ¼ tsp asafoetida, ½ tsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp ghee

## Method

1. Whisk the yoghurt smooth on its own, then whisk in the tamarind, pomegranate, lemon juice, spices, both gingers, salt, and oil.  
2. Cook in a wide, heavy, non-reactive pan over low heat, stirring often, until it thickens and reduces by about half — about 20 minutes in a wide pan, longer in a narrow one. Do not let it boil.  
3. Add the meat and keep cooking until the sauce is very thick and coats the pieces.  
4. Take the pan off the heat and stir through the asafoetida and cumin seeds, bloomed in the hot ghee.
