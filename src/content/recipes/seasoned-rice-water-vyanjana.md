Two verses that are a definition rather than a procedure: every element is a past participle agreeing with "water", and there is no cooking verb anywhere in them. A *vyañjana* is a relish — the thing eaten *with* the staple — so despite sitting in the middle of the chapter's run of drinks, this is closer to a thin sweet-sour sauce for rice than to something served in a cup.

## Ingredients

* 500 ml water from the second washing of rice, or 450 ml water plus 1 tsp rice flour  
* 20 g tamarind concentrate  
* 75 ml *takra* or cultured buttermilk  
* 30 g sugar  
* ¼ tsp ground cardamom  
* 15 ml ginger juice  
* ¼ tsp asafoetida, for the smoke  
* A small piece of lump charcoal  
* ¼ tsp salt (modern balancing addition)

## Method

1. Bring the rice water to a bare simmer for 2 minutes, then cool it completely. This is a modern step — see the notes.  
2. Whisk in the tamarind, buttermilk, sugar, cardamom, ginger juice, and salt.  
3. Fumigate. Stand a small metal cup in the middle of the bowl so its rim clears the surface, drop in a glowing piece of charcoal, sprinkle the asafoetida over it with a few drops of ghee, and cover tightly for 4–5 minutes. Lift the cup out.  
4. Serve cool, spooned over rice, or drink it cold.

**Notes**

* **The heating is mine, not the text's.** 1578–79 contains no verb of cooking; the whole thing is a cold assembly. Rice-rinse water is raw, though, so a brief simmer is prudent, and it also cooks out the raw-starch taste (necessary if you are using the rice-flour substitute). Do it to the rice water *alone* and cool it before anything else goes in: heating the finished mixture would risk splitting the buttermilk against the tamarind and would cook off the fresh-ginger character.  
* **`धूपितं ... सम्यग्`** — "thoroughly fumigated". The intensifier had been dropped, and it matters, because it confirms the asafoetida is smoked rather than stirred in. See the *Mastu* notes for the chapter's three separate constructions for asafoetida; blooming it in hot ghee is the usual modern stand-in.  
* **`ईषत्तक्रेण`** — "with a *little takra*". The *takra* is the one defined two verses earlier at 1572: churned curd cut with a quarter its volume of water. 75 ml in 500 ml is the "little".  
* **`व्यञ्जन` is not one of the five classes of food.** The scheme at 1344 is `भोज्यं भक्ष्यं पेयं लेह्यं चोष्यं` — chewed, morsels, drunk, licked, sucked — and *vyañjana* belongs to a different axis: it is the relish or accompaniment that goes with the staple. 1592–94 lists what royal rice is eaten with, and that is the company this belongs in. It is also the only occurrence of the word in the chapter.  
* **Sugar quantity is a guess.** `सितया सह योजितम्` gives no amount. 30 g against 20 g of tamarind concentrate reads as a sweet-sour relish; halve it if you want it to lean savoury. [Judgement call — the previous draft had 15 g.]  
* **The salt is an addition and the omission may be deliberate.** Both flanking entries specify salt — `सैन्धवैः` at 1575, `लवणेन` at 1580 — so this chapter salts when it means to, and 1578–79 does not.  
* Use rice-washing water collected after a first rinse that removes dust.
