The base is the liquid that runs *out* of the cloth, not the curd that stays in it — a clarified sweet whey, filtered over and over on a cloth stretched between two stakes until it runs clear. The last line files the result under `पेय`, drink, the third of the five classes of food set out at 1344, and the chapter's closing serving order at 1596 has the king drink the *pānaka*, lick the *śikhariṇī*, and suck the *majjikā*: three verbs, three classes, in order.

## Ingredients

* 1 L whole milk  
* 60 ml sour whey or *mastu*, or 40 ml lemon juice  
* 80 g sugar  
* ½ tsp ground cardamom  
* 25 g ripe tamarind pulp  
* 300 ml strained fruit juice — the text names no fruit at all  
* Cold water as needed

## Method

1. Line a sieve with a double layer of clean muslin, or stretch the cloth over a frame with a bowl beneath it.  
2. Bring the milk to a boil, take it off the heat, and stir in the sour agent until it splits cleanly and the liquid goes translucent.  
3. Stir the sugar and cardamom into the split milk, pan and all, before it goes anywhere near the cloth.  
4. Pour the whole thing onto the cloth and let it drain. Gather the cloth and press the curd firmly to force out the rest of the liquid.  
5. Pour the drained liquid back through the cloth, and keep doing it until it runs clear. To get it genuinely clear, bring it back to a boil first: the remaining whey proteins flocculate and the cloth then catches them.  
6. Dry-roast the tamarind pulp in a pan until it is dark and smells toasted, steep it in 100 ml of the warm whey, and strain. Add just enough to take the whey to a pale amber.  
7. Cool completely, mix with the fruit juice, and dilute with cold water to taste. Serve chilled.

**Notes**

* **The text names no fruit.** `यस्य कस्य फलस्यापि रसेन` is "with the juice of whatever fruit you like", and the naming rule is stated outright: the drink takes the name of the fruit. Pomegranate, mango, grape, and lime are all mine. This is the same open-ended construction the chapter uses at 1455 (`पलान्यन्यानि कानिचित्`, "some other meats").  
* **The sugar and cardamom go into the split milk, before the cloth.** All three participles in 1581–82 — `भेदितम्`, `समायुक्तम्`, `विमिश्रितम्` — agree with `क्षीरम्`, and only then does `क्षिपेत् प्रसारिते वस्त्रे` throw it onto the cloth. Doing it in this order makes the cardamom a genuine infusion whose solids the cloth then removes, which is how the liquid ends up both clear and scented.  
* **The tamarind is for colour only.** `वर्णार्थं` says so, and that fixes the quantity: enough to brown the whey, not enough to sour it. `भृष्टं` means roasted, and it has to go properly dark to do any colouring work. The sourness in the finished drink is the fruit juice's job.  
* **The coagulant is unspecified, and sour whey is the likeliest.** `अम्लेन भेदितम्` names no acid. The chapter's own curdled-milk recipe at 1408 uses `तक्रमम्लं`, sour *takra*, and *mastu* has just been defined two verses before this one at 1575 — so a sour dairy coagulant is what is nearest to hand, and it gives a milder whey than lemon does.  
* **`पेयम्`** is a technical classification, not a synonym for "drink" — see 1344 and the serving order at 1596. `पानकम् पेयमुत्तमम्` places the dish in the class and praises it in the same breath.  
* **Clarity is the point of the repeated filtering.** `पुनः पुनः क्षिपेत् ... यावन्निर्मलतां व्रजेत्` is the same operation the sugar-refining passage describes at 1413, `गालयेत् ... मुहुर्मुहुः`, and the reason for it is that raw whey is cloudy with curd fines and albumin, which look wrong in a clear fruit drink.  
* **The curds are not waste.** Sweetened, drained, pressed curd is exactly the starting point of the *kṣīra-prakāra* sweets at 1408–10, which tie the thickened mass in cloth, grind it with rice flour, fry it in ghee, and drench it in syrup.  
* `शङ्कुद्वयं` is two stakes and `यष्टिभिः` is rods: a purpose-built straining frame, not a sieve. `उज्ज्वल` is "bright" — the same fussiness about clean white cloth that runs through 1352, 1413, 1427, and 1587.
