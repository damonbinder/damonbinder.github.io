This is the end of a chain that runs 1565 → 1568 → 1571 → 1576: reduce the milk, set it to *dadhi*, churn the *dadhi* and lift off the butter, then clarify that butter — `नवनीतं नवं`, the *new* butter. The verses in between are not steps in the chain but parallel things you can do with the same curd instead. What 1577 does not say is why the wheat and the betel leaf go in. This chapter states its purposes when it has one, so the silence is worth respecting rather than filling.

## Ingredients

* 500 g unsalted cultured butter  
* 1 tsp wheat grains  
* 1 fresh edible betel leaf, washed and dried

## Method

1. Melt the butter in a heavy saucepan over low heat.  
2. Continue cooking gently until the water has boiled off, the milk solids have gone golden and settled, and the fat is clear.  
3. Throw in the wheat grains and the betel leaf, take the pan off the heat, and leave them in the hot fat for 2–3 minutes.  
4. Strain through fine cloth into a dry jar.

**Notes**

* **The butter is the butter from three verses earlier.** `नवनीतं नवं` is a back-reference to `नवनीतमथो हरेत्` at 1571, where the butter is lifted off the churn on the way to making *takra*. Cultured butter is therefore the right modern starting point, not sweet-cream butter.  
* **`गोधूमबीजकम्` is wheat grain as a material, not one seed.** The `-क` is the same self-referring suffix the chapter puts on `गोधूमचूर्णकं`, "wheat flour", at 1376. A single grain would do nothing to 500 g of fat; a teaspoon toasts in the residual heat and smells of it. [Judgement call — the strictly literal reading is "a wheat grain", singular, and if that is right the step is a doneness indicator rather than a flavouring.]  
* **The text gives no purpose for either addition.** Elsewhere this chapter says what a thing is for: `वर्णार्थं` at 1583 (tamarind, for colour), `गन्धार्थं` and `रुच्यर्थं` at 1445 (for aroma, for relish). Here it says nothing, so aroma, a doneness test, and an auspicious finish are all live and none should be asserted.  
* ***Tāmbūla*** is the betel leaf, *Piper betle* — the paan leaf, and secure; Someśvara devotes a separate section of the *Mānasollāsa* to *tāmbūla*. Use culinary betel leaf from a reliable source, not an ornamental plant.  
* **`नीरलेशविवर्जितम्` is a separate requirement from `धौतं`, not its result.** Washing butter means kneading it in cold water to rinse out residual buttermilk; the water then has to be worked out again. The point of insisting on it is that butter entering the pot dry clarifies faster and spits less. Commercial unsalted butter needs no washing, but it still carries about 16% water, which is what step 2 drives off.  
* `घृतभाण्डके` — a dedicated ghee-pot. `सम्यक्` ("properly") attaches to the heating, and `मृदुना` ("gentle") to the fire: this is a slow clarification, not a fast one.
