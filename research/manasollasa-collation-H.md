# Mānasollāsa 3.13.1565–1600 — collation against Shrigondekar (part H)

*Created 2026-07-25 13:38.*

## Summary

**I reached the edition and read every verse in my range off the printed page.** Shrigondekar, *Mānasollāsa*, Gaekwad's Oriental Series, Vol. II, Baroda 1939 — printed pages **133–136**, which carry verses 1561–1600 and the chapter colophon. Two independent scans, checked against each other: the DLI PDF at `https://www.ebharatisampat.in/pdfs/10403-Manasollasa.pdf` (printed page + 49 = PDF page, so pp. 133–136 are PDF pp. 182–185; the embedded page images are 600 dpi 1-bit, and I read them at native resolution rather than re-rendering), and the greyscale scan at `https://archive.org/details/EhVx_manasollasa-of-king-someshwara-vol-2-by-gk-shri-gondekar-baroda-1939-oriental-in` (leaf = printed page + 56, so leaves 189–192; native only ~1687 px wide, lower resolution than the DLI images, but greyscale, which settles closed-vs-open bowls that 1-bit thresholding fills in).

**Eleven Devanagari corrections, of which six are substantive** — they change what the text says, not just how it is spelled. Four of the six were invisible to a metrical check; two were the metrical defects the brief flagged. Before correction the repo text had **67 of 73 hemistichs at exactly 16 syllables**; the corrected text has **73 of 73**. Every defect turned out to be our error, not the edition's, exactly as the pattern in the other ranges predicted.

**The most consequential findings.** (1) The `mastu` recipe at 1575 has **fresh ginger, not ghee** — `जीरकार्द्रकसैन्धवैः`, cumin/ginger/rock salt; our `जीरकाज्य` is the 7-syllable pāda the brief flagged, and it invented an ingredient. (2) The meal's last course at 1597 is **`तक्रान्नम्`, takra-*rice*, not plain takra** — which is why the verb is `अश्नीयात्`, "he should eat", and not `पिबेत्`; our `तक्रम्` was a syllable short and turned a dish into a drink. (3) `मुद्र` at 1589 **stands in the print** with no variant recorded — the brief's `मुद्ग`/mung hypothesis is *not* confirmed, and I have not emended it. (4) The chapter's closing word `यद्भूपो अन्नभोगः` is printed **with the hiatus unelided**, and the hiatus is metrically required; our `यद्भूपोऽन्नभोगः` was 15 syllables.

**What the unreviewed 1585–1600 turned out to contain.** It is not filler. 1585–1588 is a **service inventory**: gold or silver dishes on a brass frame (`रीतियन्त्र`), a gold `कच्चोल` cup, iron `गङ्गलक` vessels, gold `पिङ्गालक`s, shell-shaped spoons, gold platters, all washed and wiped with white cloth. 1589–1591 gives the four **directions of eating** and their rewards (east → long life, south → fame, west → prosperity, north → the fruit of truthful speech). 1592–1594 lists what the rice is eaten with, and includes a compound our text had wrong: **`खारखण्ड`, "saline pieces", not `खरखण्ड`**. And 1595–1597 is **the structural close of the whole food section**: `पिबेत्` / `लिह्यात्` / `चूषेत` — drink, lick, suck — run the last three of the five food classes announced at 1344 (`भोज्यं भक्ष्यं तथा पेयं लेह्यं चोष्यं`) in their own order, ending with takra-rice, then milk or `काञ्जिक`. The digest on the site says none of this.

**1600 is the end of the food section, confirmed.** Printed p. 136 gives 1600, then the half-verse colophon `अन्नभोगः समाख्यातः सोमेश्वरमहीभुजा ।` and `॥ इत्यन्नभोगः ॥ १३ ॥`. What follows immediately on the same page is **Adhyāya 14, the Pānīyabhoga** — the enjoyment of drink — opening `हृद्यः पानीयभोगोऽयमिदानीमभिधीयते ॥ १ ॥`, with the **verse numbering restarting at 1**. The running head changes from `[ अध्यायः १३` to `[ अध्यायः १४`. So 1342–1600 is the complete extent of the food chapter and nothing is missing after it.

**Two cruxes are in the edition, not in us**, and I have carried them rather than smoothing them: 1571b, where Shrigondekar prints `उदस्वित्या` with his own parenthesized correction `(श्विच्च)`, and 1587a, where the print reads `भंम-`, which is not a word. Details below. **I emended nothing by conjecture.**

Deliverable: `research/collation/partH.json` — 15 entries, continuous 1565–1600, validated as JSON, every hemistich scanned.

## Corrections

Substantive changes are marked **S**; the rest are orthography where our text had silently normalised the edition's spelling.

## Unresolved

**1587a, `भंमशुक्तिसमोपेते`.** The first word of the vessel list is not a word. Both scans, read at native resolution and at every magnification I could get, give `भं` + `म` — `भ` with an anusvāra, then `म` (a closed left bowl, distinct from the `ग` of `गङ्गल` two lines above). Apparatus note 2 has no manuscript siglum printed and reads simply `भुम`, so the second consonant was already unstable in the witnesses. What can be said: the pāda requires **two** syllables in this slot, which rules out our `भृङ्गार` (a golden ewer — the semantically obvious word, and almost certainly what the transcription tradition was reaching for) but permits `भृंग`. I have not emended, and the JSON prints `भंम-` with a bracketed note. If anyone opens a *third* witness of this page, this is the line to read.

**1571b, `उदस्वित्या(श्विच्च)`.** Corrupt in the edition; see the table. I could not settle whether the print has `उदस्वित्या` or `उदस्वित्यां` — the mark above the `या` may be an anusvāra or may be footnote marker 16, and at 600 dpi 1-bit they are the same three pixels. The sense is secure regardless.

**1589b, `मुद्र`.** The edition prints `मुद्र` and records no variant. `मुद्ग`, mung bean, is what a reader wants, and the *d*+*ra* ligature versus *d*+*ga* is not a confusion Devanagari makes easily — but the ligature in the print is unmistakably `द्र`, with the subjoined *ra* stroke descending to the right, in both scans. **Not emended.** The translation says the edition prints `मुद्र`, that it is not a food-name, and that `मुद्ग` is the obvious sense but is not in the text.

**1594a, `खारखण्डोपखण्डकैः`.** The reading is now firm, but the compound is still not identified. `खार` is "saline, brackish"; the pairing of `खण्ड` with `उपखण्डक` looks like larger pieces and smaller ones, in a list otherwise made of *vaṭaka*s and *parpaṭa*s — so brined or salt-cured pieces of something, plausibly fish or meat, is the natural guess. It is a guess, and the translation says so. A prior agent's retracted hypothesis is neither confirmed nor refuted by the print: the print simply reads `खार`.

**Apparatus entries I could not read.** Printed p. 133 notes 2 (`D तालं / A तलं आदौ`), 16, and 17; p. 135 notes 4, 5, and 8. None of them bears on my range's substantive readings. The 1-bit DLI scan renders the footnote type near the limit of legibility, and the archive.org scan is lower resolution still.

## Conventions used in the JSON

- **Substantive readings follow the print.** Where our text and the print differ in a letter, the print wins and the table above records it.
- **Orthography follows the print** where the difference is visible in the edition (`सम्यक् मृदुना`, `सञ्छाद्य`, `किञ्चित्`, `पिंगाल`, the unelided `यद्भूपो अन्नभोगः`), with one exception: I kept `वापि` where the print sets `वाऽपि`, since the file uses `वापि` throughout and the avagraha is purely typographic. Verse markers and daṇḍa punctuation follow the existing file (`॥ ६५ ॥`, with the full number at the century: `॥ १५७० ॥`, `॥ १५८० ॥`, `॥ १५९० ॥`, `॥ १६०० ॥`).
- **The editor's own parentheses are carried through**, at 1571b and 1581b, rather than resolved silently.
- **Optatives are rendered as optatives** — "he should cook", "he should drain off" — and the epithets are kept.
- **Sense-unit blocks.** The ten refs that correspond to existing recipes (1565–1567, 1568–1570, 1571–1572, 1573, 1574, 1575, 1576–1577, 1578–1579, 1580, 1581–1584) reuse the exact `ref` strings so the page's links survive. The closing material, which has no recipe, is broken into five blocks at its own joints: **1585–1588** the vessels and setting out the meal, **1589–1591** the king seated and the four directions, **1592–1594** what the rice is eaten with, **1595–1597** the order of the courses, **1598–1600** the pairings, the seasonal diet, and the colophon. Note that 1588 straddles the first two — 1588ab finishes the serving-out, 1588cd begins the king covering himself — and the English carries the sentence across the break with an ellipsis, as the file does elsewhere.
- **Metre verified.** All 73 hemistichs of 1565–1600 plus the colophon half-verse scan at exactly 16 syllables (36 verses × 2, plus the colophon). Script at `scratchpad/H/build.py` if anyone wants to re-run it.
