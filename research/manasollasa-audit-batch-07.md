# Mānasollāsa 3.13.1499–1531 — translation and reconstruction audit (batch 7)

*Created 2026-07-25 12:14.*

Scope: the seven recipes covering 3.13.1499–1531 — `varna-sunthaka-liver-and-fat-skewers.md`, `kalakhanda-preparations.md`, `utkvathita-long-cooked-meat-soup.md`, `upakhandaka-dried-meat.md`, `spiced-roasted-marrow.md`, `kanjika-cooked-brain.md`, and the published `tamarind-fried-fish.md`. All seven were edited; nothing else in `src/` was touched, and no `original` field was altered.

## Summary

**All seven had substantive errors.** The two biggest are both in the offal run, and both are of the same kind: a specific anatomical term read as a generic one.

**The single most consequential correction: `वपा` in 1499 is the caul, the omentum — not "fat".** The recipe was built on 250 g of back fat, which cannot be what the verse describes, because 1499 calls it `वर्तिबद्धा`, "bound into a roll", wrapped with a band. You roll and tie a membrane; you do not roll a slab of back fat. Three independent checks land on the caul: the chapter has an ordinary word for fat (`मेदस्`) that it uses freely and does not use here; 1426 lists `वपा` among the parts `सुसंलग्नं`, "closely attached", inside the animal's body, in the compound `वपामस्तिष्कमज्जकम्` — caul, brain, and marrow together, soft internal tissues rather than trimmings, and incidentally the three organs of this batch's 1499, 1520–21, and 1518–19; and `वपा` is the cut the Vedic animal sacrifice roasts on a spit, so a spitted omentum is an old idea in this literature. The dish also makes far better sense this way — caul is the classic self-basting wrapper for liver, which is exactly the function the recipe's own intro claims for it.

**Second: `कालखण्ड` is the liver, securely, and two recipes said it was uncertain.** Apte glosses it simply "the liver" and gives a culinary citation; Shabda-Sagara derives it as *kāla* "black" plus *khaṇḍa* "part". Decisively, the chapter's own anatomy list at 1425 reads `कालखण्डं तथा वृक्कौ` — "the *kālakhaṇḍa* and the two kidneys" — so it is a named organ distinct from the kidney. This is the defect-13 pattern from the earlier audits: a hedge on a secure term.

**Third, and the find I'd most want checked: 1506 is in the dual and every translation of it had pluralized that away.** `समेदस्कौ द्विधा भक्तौ … लवणमिश्रितौ … तौ` — four dual forms in a row, so not a slip. Something paired, fatty, halved, steeped in sour, then fried. **The kidneys are the obvious referent** (`वृक्कौ` is dual and sits next to `कालखण्ड` at 1425; kidneys are encased in fat; halving lengthwise and soaking in acid before frying is exactly how they are handled). I've applied the kidney reading and flagged it for reversion.

**Before Damon cooks anything from this batch:**

- **The brain recipe's safety note was too soft and is now specific.** Sheep and goat skull-and-brain over twelve months old is specified risk material in the UK and EU and cannot legally be sold for food; the threshold for cattle is twelve months in the UK/EU and thirty in the US. Calf and lamb brain are fine; the adult sheep's head that 1520 actually calls for is not obtainable in either jurisdiction.
- **The marrow recipe's use of pre-split bones is a functional downgrade, not a convenience.** The text roasts the bone *whole* and cracks it afterwards, which keeps the marrow in the bone while it heats. Split bones at 230 °C leak into the tray. I dropped the oven to 220 °C and noted it.
- **The dried meat is not safe to eat off the rack.** The reconstruction is a semi-dried meat, not jerky, and all of its safety rests on the final roast. That is now said in the method, not just implied in the notes. Also: the text asks for *fatty* meat and the reconstruction quietly uses lean. Sensible, but it was undeclared.
- **The published fried fish has a real translation error that explains Damon's own cooking judgement**, and it is the nicest result in this batch. See §7.

The 1502 formula parallel **holds**, and holds more strongly than the earlier audit claimed. See §2.

The gap at 1510–1512 is **correct** — those verses are the sheep-butchery passage, not a missing recipe. See §3.

## 1. *Varṇa-Śuṇṭhaka* (1499–1501) — `varna-sunthaka-liver-and-fat-skewers.md`

### Confirmed

**V1. `वपा` is the caul, not fat.** Covered in the summary. One caveat on my own evidence: `पशुकायां` in 1426 I first read as "at the ribs" (`पर्शुका` = rib), which would have been a direct anatomical placement of the omentum, but the form is not secure and `पशु-काय`, "animal body", is the likelier parse — so the note says "inside the animal's body" and leans on the `वपा`/`मस्तिष्क`/`मज्जक` grouping instead, which is unambiguous. The recipe now leads with 250 g caul fat, rolled into a ~3 cm cylinder, tied with twine, and cut into 4 cm lengths, with back fat kept as an explicit substitute rather than as the default. The practical consequence I added to the method: rendering caul drips and flares, so the skewers have to sit off the hottest part of the fire — the reconstruction as written would have scorched the liver before the fat rendered.

**V2. The absolute measure was mis-anchored.** `अङ्गुलद्वयमानेन` (1499c) sits on the caul roll, and 1500's `तत्प्रमाणानि` then sizes the liver to match. The recipe had it the other way round: liver at 4 cm, fat "cut into similar pieces". Reversed. The measure itself checks out — at the standard `अङ्गुल` of ~1.9 cm, two finger-widths is ~3.8 cm, so 4 cm is right; and this is the one *absolute* measurement in a chapter that otherwise sizes by comparison. Both the absolute unit and its dependent `तत्प्रमाणानि` are now carried, per the brief.

**V3. `पञ्चाङ्गपट्टद्वेष्ट्या` was dropped from the translation entirely** — the wrapping instruction, i.e. the thing that tells you the caul is rolled and tied. Restored, with a bracket flagging the corruption.

**V4. `सोमभूभुजा` was dropped.** 1501d closes `वर्णितं सोमभूभुजा`, "described by King Soma" — the author signing his own recipe in the third person. Restored, with a note that Someśvara III is the author, so this is a convention rather than a citation.

**V5. The `कालखण्ड` hedge.** Removed; see the summary.

### Judgement call

**J-V1. `वर्ण` = colour.** The existing intro's claim that the name refers to the contrast of dark liver against white fat is correct and I kept it, strengthening it with the cross-reference to `पञ्चवर्णी` at 1491, the chapter's other colour-named dish. `वर्ण` can also mean "kind, class", but this chapter demonstrably uses it for colour in a dish name.

### Unresolved

**U-V1. `पञ्चाङ्गपट्टद्वेष्ट्या` (1499a).** Seven syllables against eight, and `द्वेष्ट्या` is not a form. Sense requires something built on `वेष्ट्-`, "to wrap". **Reading `पञ्चाङ्गुल-` for `पञ्चाङ्ग-` would restore the count exactly** and fit a verse that measures in `अङ्गुल`s two pādas later — a five-finger-wide band cut into two-finger lengths. That is a conjecture, so it is in the notes as a checkable suggestion and the Devanagari is untouched. This is the only defective pāda in my whole batch outside the fish passage; **recommend collating 1499 against Shrigondekar's GOS edition** before publication.

**U-V2. `शूलपोत` (1500c).** I expected this to be a slip for `शूलप्रोत` ("pierced on a spit"), which is the standard word. It isn't: **`शूलपोतानि कृत्वा तान् अङ्गारेषु प्रतापयेत्` recurs verbatim at 1464**, inside the published *Bhaditraka Skewers*. Two identical attestations in one chapter make it a real word in this witness, and the metre doesn't distinguish the two readings. Recorded as a negative result — do not emend.

### Files edited

`src/content/recipes/varna-sunthaka-liver-and-fat-skewers.md`. Title `Varṇa-Śuṇṭhaka Liver-and-Fat Skewers` → `*Varṇa-Śuṇṭhaka* Liver-and-Caul Skewers` (asterisks per CLAUDE.md, "Fat" → "Caul" per V1). `related: [kalakhanda-preparations]` added — 1502 chains off this recipe (see K3).

## 2. *Kālakhaṇḍa* preparations (1502–1506) — and the independent verdict on the 1502 formula

### The formula parallel: verdict, holds, with two corrections to the earlier audit

**It holds, and it is stronger than the earlier audit made it.** Three points, two of which the earlier audit didn't have:

1. **The word sequence is identical, slot for slot.** 1449a–b `चणकस्य समान् खण्डान् कल्पयित्वा विचक्षणः`; 1502c–d `पूगीफलप्रमाणेन खण्डान् कृत्वा विचक्षणः`. [size standard] + `खण्डान्` + [absolutive of "make"] + `विचक्षणः`, down to the pāda-final word.
2. **The material is indeed named in the preceding pāda and omitted from the formula.** 1502b is `कालखण्डं विकृत्य च` — `कालखण्डं` in the accusative as the object of `विकृत्य`. Exactly the ellipsis being claimed for 1449.
3. **The new point: in 1502 the material reading is not merely unlikely, it is impossible.** `पूगीफल` is the areca or betel nut. Nobody oils, peppers, salts, and fumigates pieces of areca nut. So 1502 *must* be [size] + `खण्डान्` + `कृत्वा` + `विचक्षणः` with the material elided, which means the construction exists in this chapter independently of anything one wants to argue about 1449. The earlier audit treated 1502 as a parallel; it is better than that — it is a control case where the competing reading is ruled out on non-linguistic grounds.

Supporting: `प्रमाण` as a size marker is used five times in this stretch, adjectivally at 1453 (`तत्प्रमाणान्`), 1473 (`पूगीफलप्रमाणानि`), 1477 (`तत्प्रमाणकैः`), and 1500 (`तत्प्रमाणानि`), and instrumentally here. And **1473 is a third, independent attestation of areca nut specifically as a ruler**: `पूगीफलप्रमाणानि कृत्वा खण्डानि पूर्ववत्`, where `पूर्ववत्` back-references the earlier sizings.

**Two corrections to the earlier audit's citation.** It cites the line as "1502b". It is **1502c–d** — `पूगीफलप्रमाणेन` fills a whole pāda by itself at 8 syllables, so the pāda boundaries of the two lines don't coincide even though the word order does (in 1449 `खण्डान्` closes pāda a; in 1502 it opens pāda d). The argument is unaffected — it rests on word order, not on pāda position — but the surface alignment is not quite as tidy as "slot for slot down to the same pāda" implies, and it's worth saying so before the claim goes into a published note.

**And yes, `kalakhanda-preparations.md` carries the areca-nut measure** — but only in the `translation` field. The method said "cut into bite-sized pieces", losing the one dimension the verse gives. Now 2 cm.

### Confirmed

**K1. `चक्कलिकाः` flattened to "thin slices" (1505).** 1505b is `कृत्वा चक्कलिकाः शुभाः` — **word for word identical to 1436b**, which defines the shape as `ताडपत्रसमाकाराः`, "of the same shape as a palmyra leaf". So this is the liver version of the four published pork *Cakkalikās*, and the earlier translation dropped both the technical term and the shape, plus the epithet `शुभाः`. Restored, and the method now cuts the citron version as 3 mm × 5–7 cm strips, matching the published *Cakkalikās*.

**K2. 1506 is dual.** See the summary. Applied and flagged.

**K3. Two back-references dropped.** 1502a `अङ्गारेषु तथा भृष्ट्वा` — "roasted over embers **in the same way**", pointing at 1500 — and 1504a `अनेन विधिना`, "by this method". Neither survived, which is why step 1 opened from a pan-sear with no indication that the text sends you back to the previous recipe's ember roast. Both restored; step 1 rewritten; `related` added in both directions.

**K4. `हिङ्गुधूपेन धूपयेत्` flattened into a dry spice.** 1503d is fumigation. The ingredient list had ¼ tsp asafoetida sitting among the dry powders and the method tossed it in with them. Now a hot-ghee bloom poured over as a separate step, matching how the site's *Roast-Meat Salad* handles the same instruction.

**K5. The `कालखण्ड` hedge.** Removed; see the summary.

### Judgement calls

**J-K1. `अजाजी` = cumin, not "cumin or caraway".** The *Rājanighaṇṭu* (6.58–59) lists `अजाजी` among the synonyms of `श्वेतजीरक`, white cumin — *Cuminum cyminum*. That is the same thirteenth-century thesaurus the earlier audit used for `विश्वा` = dried ginger, so within a century of this text. Caraway is `कारवी` or `कृष्णजीरक`. The chapter uses plain `जीरक` at 1495 and 1542, so this is synonym variation of exactly the kind already documented for ginger (`नागर` / `शुण्ठी` / `विश्व`) and turmeric (`निशा` / `हरिद्रा`). Applied here and in the marrow recipe. `अजाजी` appears in both, as the brief noted.

**J-K2. The sour-fried variant's soak volume.** `आम्लकैर्भावयित्वा` is *steeping* — `भावना` is a technical term for saturating a substance in a liquid. 30 ml of tamarind water steeps nothing. Raised to 200 ml with a 30-minute soak.

### Unresolved

**U-K1. What `समेदस्कौ` refers to.** Kidneys at roughly 75%, on the evidence in the summary. The live alternative is that these are simply fatty pieces of the liver and the dual has some referent I can't recover. I've applied kidneys and flagged step 6 explicitly so it can be reverted in one edit.

### Files edited

`src/content/recipes/kalakhanda-preparations.md`. Title asterisked. Intro rewritten (it described five liver preparations; there are four, and the fifth is probably not liver). `related: [varna-sunthaka-liver-and-fat-skewers]`.

## 3. What occupies 1510–1512

**Sheep butchery, and no recipe is missing.** 1510–1512 opens the sheep's belly, removes the viscera, binds the hind legs, ties the head with a rope and singes it over flame `यावद्रोमाणि गच्छन्ति यावत्कृष्णत्वमेति च` ("until the hair is gone and it turns black"), washes it, and then says `शेषं क्रोडवदाचरेत्` — "treat the rest as with the pig", back-referencing the pig procedure at 1427–1431 — and `अन्येषां श्वापदानां च शेषं मेषवदाचरेत्`, "and for other wild animals, treat the rest as with the sheep".

This is the ovine counterpart of 1427–1431, which is likewise not a standalone recipe (it is the head of `roast-pork-sunthaka.md`). **1522–1523 is the same kind of passage for birds and also has no recipe file**, so the pattern is consistent and deliberate: carcass-preparation passages don't get pages. The gap is correct and needs no action.

One oddity, noted only in passing since it is outside my files and in a passage I must not edit: 1510a's `विशैस्तस्य च मेषस्य` is rendered "with a knife" in `manasollasa.ts`, but `विश` is not a cutting implement in the lexica (the root for slaughter is `विशस्`, and `विशा` is dried ginger). The half-line scans clean at 16, so it isn't a metrical flag, but the instrumental is unexplained.

## 4. *Utkvāthita* soup (1507–1509) — `utkvathita-long-cooked-meat-soup.md`

### Confirmed

**S1. `स्थाल्यवक्त्रे` dropped entirely** (1508a). Whatever the compound is, it restricts the vessel's mouth — read as `स्थाली` + `अवक्त्र` it is a narrow-necked pot, read as a *bahuvrīhi* it is a pot covered with a `स्थाली`. Either way the method's "partly covered" is wrong for a 3–4 hour simmer of 3 L: it would reduce a long way past a `सूप`. Changed to covered, with the ambiguity in the notes.

**S2. `ख्यातं शास्त्रविशारदैः` dropped** (1509d) — "renowned among those skilled in the treatises". Worth keeping because it contrasts with 1501, where the king claims a dish as his own: here he is citing dietetic authority instead.

### Judgement calls

**J-S1. "Concentrated" in the intro.** Not in the text and not true of a 1.5 kg : 3 L broth. Changed to "long-simmered" — one word, minimal.

**J-S2. The acid timing is faithful but needs a warning.** 1508 puts the asafoetida, the sour agent, and the salt in with the meat and water *before* 1509's long boil, so the reconstruction's front-loaded tamarind is exactly right. But acid slows collagen→gelatin conversion, so the method should expect the long end of 3–4 hours and not increase the tamarind. Per the brief's instruction (validated cooking vs. differing source), **I did not change the method** — I added a note saying that holding half the sourness back is the modern fix and is a departure.

### Unresolved

**U-S1. `स्थाल्यवक्त्र`.** Both readings given, neither asserted. Also minor: `अंसकीकस` is specifically the *shoulder* bone (`कीकस` = bone, rib, vertebra), where the earlier translation had "adjacent bones"; the whole 1507 cut list is now spelled out as forequarter-on-the-bone.

Kept as correct: the existing note that `उत्क्वाथित` is a decoction term. It is — `उत्-क्वथ्`, the `क्वाथ` family, and `काथयेत्` is the chapter's word for simmering in liquid elsewhere too (1457). Sharpened by adding that the endpoint the text names is the meat's tenderness (`मार्दवं`), not a degree of reduction.

## 5. *Upakhaṇḍaka* dried meat (1513–1517) — `upakhandaka-dried-meat.md`

### Confirmed

**P1. `चक्कलीः` flattened to "cut thin"** (1516b). Same term as 1505 and 1436 — palmyra-leaf-shaped, long flat tapering slices. Restored. It is also, incidentally, the right geometry for drying, so this is a case where the technical term was carrying useful cooking information.

**P2. Three epithets dropped, two of them technical.** 1515d `सर्वशाकोत्तमानि` — "the best of all `शाक`s". `शाक` is normally a *vegetable* dish in this chapter (1548 subdivides it into fruit-, leaf-, tuber-, flower-, and pod-`शाक`, and 1549 says vegetables are to be prepared `मांसवत्`, "like meat"), so applying it to a dried meat is either a loose "side dish" or a deliberate boast. 1517c–d `हृद्यान् पथ्यान् सुगन्धांश्च`: **`पथ्य` is the Ayurvedic dietetic category of the wholesome**, the opposite of `अपथ्य`, not a synonym for "nice". This is the same drop the published `bhaditraka-skewers.md` has at 1465 (`रुच्यं लघु पथ्यं मनोहरम्`, all four gone) — a chapter-wide pattern in these drafts, worth a sweep.

**P3. The safety framing understated the dependency.** The reconstruction is correct to refuse the source's ambient shade-drying, and correct that half the weight gone is not shelf-stable (that needs closer to two-thirds). But there is no nitrite, no acidification, and no smoke, and 65–70 °C oven air will not bring thin strips much above 60–65 °C because of evaporative cooling. **All of the safety is in step 4's final roast**, and the "swell and become tasty" endpoint in the translation actively tempts you to eat them off the rack. Step 3 now says so, and adds a one-week limit on the refrigerated hold.

### Judgement calls

**J-P1. The text says fatty, the recipe uses lean, and nobody said so.** 1513a is `समेदस्कानि मांसानि`. Rendered fat oxidizes fast and there is no cure here to protect it, so lean is the right call — and the text's own second version at 1516 switches to `हरिण`, deer, which is naturally very lean, so lean is at least inside the passage. Kept lean, declared the departure, flagged.

**J-P2. Salt 25 g → 30 g** (2.5% → 3%), towards 1516's `प्राज्यलवणेन`, "with abundant salt".

**J-P3. Strips 1 cm → 5 mm.** At 1 cm the surface case-hardens before the middle gives up its water. The text says only `दीर्घाणि`, "long", and specifies no thickness, so this is a cooking judgement rather than a philological one.

Also: the pepper and cumin are a reading of `सम्भार` ("requisites, seasonings", 1516c), which is the only thing the passage says about them. **No spice but asafoetida and salt is named in 1513–1517.** Noted, since it's a reconstruction invention downstream of a generic term.

### Unresolved

**U-P1. `उपखण्डक` is not in the lexica** — wisdomlib returns nothing. It appears to be this text's own coinage, "sub-piece". Left transliterated, which the recipe already did correctly.

## 6. Marrow (1518–1519) and brain (1520–1521)

### Marrow — `spiced-roasted-marrow.md`

**M1. Confirmed: pre-split bones are a functional downgrade.** The text roasts the `नलक` **whole** in the fire and bursts it with stone blows *afterwards*. That order keeps the marrow inside the bone while it heats. The recipe's note treated the swap as pure convenience; it isn't, and 230 °C on split bones will run the marrow into the tray. Dropped to 220 °C and the note rewritten. If Damon can get whole shanks and is willing to hit them, the source's order genuinely yields more.

**M2. Confirmed: four animals, not two.** `रुरुशम्बरसराङ्गच्छागस्य` = `रुरु` deer, sambar (`शम्बर` — the source of the English word, *Rusa unicolor*), `सारङ्ग` deer, goat. These are the same three deer the chapter grades for eating quality at 1417–1419, so this is a coherent internal cross-reference and beef is a modern substitution the text does not offer. Restored.

**M3. Confirmed: `प्रयत्नतः` and `विचक्षणः` dropped.** The second is formulaic in this chapter (1449, 1502, 1519) and the site keeps it elsewhere.

**Cooking stress-test.** 1 kg of bones yields 150–250 g of marrow, which is 85–90% fat. 20 ml of aqueous acid into that will not emulsify — it needs to go in off the heat, be served immediately, and will separate on standing. Added as a note. Food safety: femur and tibia marrow is not specified risk material in any jurisdiction (spinal cord and vertebral column are), and `नलक` + `पृथु` selects exactly the long bones. Noted, so a reader doesn't have to wonder.

**J-M1.** `अजाजी` → cumin, as at 1503. **J-M2.** 230 °C → 220 °C.

### Brain — `kanjika-cooked-brain.md`

**B1. Confirmed: the spice order is inverted and the fumigation was lost.** 1521 runs `पुनः पचेत्` (cook again), *then* `चूर्णैः संयोज्य तत् पश्चात्` ("having combined it with the powders, afterwards"), *then* `हिङ्गुधूपेन धूपयेत्`. The method heated the spices in the ghee before the brain went in, with the asafoetida among them as a dry spice — inverting both the order and the technique, since `धूपयेत्` is a final aromatic and is literally the last word of the recipe. Reordered: fry, then toss with the powders off the heat, then a separate asafoetida bloom poured over.

**B2. Confirmed: the safety note was too soft.** Rewritten with the actual rule; see the summary. Also noted that brain runs around 2 g of cholesterol per 100 g, which matters at a 500 g serving.

**J-B1.** `चूर्णैः` is bare, "with the powders", so the specific spices are the reconstructor's. The turmeric has no warrant in the passage at all — noted rather than removed, since 1503's pepper/cumin/salt is a reasonable place to read them from and turmeric is not implausible in the chapter.

**Cooking stress-test: the poach is sound.** `काञ्जिक` is a mildly acidic fermented rice liquor, and mild acid firms brain protein — the same reason French practice poaches brains in a vinegar court-bouillon. 500 ml for 500 g just covers; 8–10 minutes gently is right. The cold-salted-water soak and membrane removal are not in the text but are standard and sensible; now said so rather than left to look like source material.

Both passages scan clean (4/4 half-lines each).

## 7. Fried fish (1524–1531) — the published file, with a verbatim change log

Damon's judgement about which steps to skip is untouched — the intro, the ingredient quantities, the `*(Optional)*` markers, and all five method steps are exactly as they were. Everything below is either the `translation` field, a typo, or an additive `## Notes` section (which the other published Mānasollāsa recipes all have).

### The result that matters: the rub was mistranslated, and the correct reading explains why it did nothing

1527 reads `मत्स्यानां घर्षणं कार्यं तैलेन लवणेन च । यावत्पिच्छलतां याति मत्स्यगन्धश्च नश्यति`.

`यावत् पिच्छलतां याति` is "**until it goes to** sliminess" — accusative with `याति`, so the sliminess is *attained*. Only the second clause is a destruction (`मत्स्यगन्धश्च नश्यति`, "and the fishy smell is destroyed"). The published translation had "until the slime and fish smell disappear", which reverses the first clause and applies one verb to both nouns.

This is not a pedantic point. Salt plus friction lifts the mucus off fish skin, which makes the surface *slippery*; the turmeric wash at 1528 is what then removes it, and 1528's press-in-cloth is what dries it. That is a coherent three-step protocol for **whole scaled fish** — the subject 1524 announces, `सशल्कानां च मत्स्यानां`. **On skinless fillets there is no mucus layer to lift, so the step has nothing to work on.** Damon's "didn't add much" is exactly what the correct reading predicts for the ingredient he used. His judgement stands and is now explained rather than merely recorded.
